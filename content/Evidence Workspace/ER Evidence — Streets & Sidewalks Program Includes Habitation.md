# ER Evidence — The Streets & Sidewalks Program Includes Habitation of the Unhoused

Record evidence supporting the inference that the City of Berkeley's streets-and-sidewalks program (its "service, program, or activity" under ADA Title II) encompasses habitation by unhoused people. Cites use the `volume-ER-page` format. Quotes verified against extracted text; page numbers marked **[OCR]** came from scanned pages — verify wording against the Source PDF before quoting in the brief.

---

## I. The district court expressly so held (strongest evidence)

The April 3, 2026 summary judgment order (Dkt. 288, [[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…|1-ER-97–170]]) *defines* the program to include habitation:

- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-124|1-ER-124]]** — the core holding: "The relevant program is: the City's maintenance of public roadways and sidewalks to ensure their functionality, orderly use, and public health and safety. **This includes use of roadways and sidewalks which serve as locations of abode for the unhoused.**" Same page: streets and sidewalks "have long functioned to serve numerous purposes (e.g., facilitating transit and movement, permitting speech and protests, enhancing urban aesthetics, **serving as places of abode for the unhoused**, etc.)," and adopting *L.A. Alliance*: "Skid Row area sidewalks are a service, program, or activity of the City within the meaning of Title II." 14 F.4th 947, 959 (9th Cir. 2021).
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-125|1-ER-125]]** — "The definition also encompasses the fact that City streets and sidewalks **have long been used as places of abode for the unhoused**. The City's management of the program thus must consider... the effects on unhoused disabled residents who... seek to occupy portions of the public right-of-way."
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-160|1-ER-160]]** — the court twice describes the program as "the City's **program of administering safe and healthy streets in their use by the unhoused**," and orders tent/sleeping-gear replacement as a reasonable accommodation *within* that program — rejecting the City's argument that replacing tents "is the equivalent of sanctioning camping on public property."
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-123|1-ER-123]]** — frames the program-definition dispute (*Where Do We Go Berkeley* narrow vs. *L.A. Alliance* broad) and resolves it in favor of the broad municipal framing.
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-135|1-ER-135]]** — refers to "the program of maintaining the streets" whose operation abatement decisions are part of.

## II. The City's own regulations treat sidewalk lodging as permitted-but-regulated, not banned

- **[[ER 0483-0491 - Exhibit H – Email Thread for Jonny Ruel Holder#ER-489|3-ER-489]]** — City's ADA determination letter (Gregory) [OCR-adjacent; verify]: "**In limited circumstances, individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding nine square feet**, provided pedestrian access is maintained, facilities are not obstructed, and **the location is not designated as a no-lodging area**." Also: "its encampment regulations are reflected in municipal code provisions, administrative regulations, and City Council resolutions that apply citywide." A program that designates specific "no-lodging areas" necessarily contemplates lodging elsewhere.
- **[[ER 0483-0491 - Exhibit H – Email Thread for Jonny Ruel Holder#ER-490|3-ER-490]]** — same letter: the City "cannot provide a map identifying areas where tents are categorically permitted" because "enforcement decisions depend on specific, evolving conditions **rather than fixed geographic boundaries**" — camping is condition-managed, not geographically prohibited.
- **[[ER 1295-1306 - Declaration of David White ISO of City of Berkeley’s Motion for Summar…#ER-1303|6-ER-1303]]** — Administrative Regulation 10.2 itself (Ex. to David White Decl., Dkt. 219-5): "TNC Objects" are *prohibited* on sidewalks only in Residential Districts; in **Commercial and Manufacturing Districts** they are permitted subject to conditions ("shall not be left unattended for more than 2 hours," path-of-travel, etc.). A district-by-district permission scheme regulates — and thereby includes — the presence of dwellings and belongings on sidewalks.
- **[[ER 1380-1423 - Joint Status Report#ER-1388|7-ER-1388]]** — City in Joint Status Report (Dkt. 194): "Administrative Regulation 10.2 establishes **size-limited sidewalk use requirements that are fundamental to the City's sidewalk management program** and apply to all individuals using City sidewalks... including the 9-square-foot footprint and related placement restrictions... designed to ensure that sidewalk use remains temporary and compatible with shared public access." The City's own description: individual "sidewalk use" with structures, capped at 9 sq. ft., is a "fundamental operational feature" of the program.
- **[[ER 1380-1423 - Joint Status Report#ER-1393|7-ER-1393]]** — City denies a 10'×12' tent as "an individualized exemption from **the City's size limits for sidewalk structures**" — size limits for sidewalk structures presuppose structures on sidewalks.
- **[[ER 1369-1371 - Order Setting Summary Judgment Briefing Schedule#ER-1370|7-ER-1370]]** — Order Setting SJ Briefing (Dkt. 202): the court directs the City "to explain why **permitting tents of 10x12 feet or 20x30 feet** would fundamentally alter the City's sidewalk management program," treating tent-permitting as a question *within* the program's parameters.

## III. The City's on-record representations: no camping ban, council-directed non-enforcement

- **[[ER 0833-0837 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Motion for TRO#ER-835|4-ER-835]]** — Order re TRO (Dkt. 187, 3/7/26): "The Court also notes the City's representation on the record that **it does not maintain a city-wide blanket ban on sleeping outdoors**, and that certain conduct and space-restriction regulations, such as the 3x3 space occupation rule, which might make camping on the streets and sidewalks impracticable **are not administratively enforced per the City Council** absent certain defined circumstances."
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-141|1-ER-141]]** — SJ order: the City "represents that **it does not have a general policy barring residents from sleeping in public places**, and there is no apparent policy limiting the number of unhoused persons from sleeping in any given area."
- **[[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-23|1-ER-23]]** — City counsel (Azizi), 3/20/26 hearing: "the City doesn't have a program of just removing individuals from the street. **It is part of a broader program** as the Court noted in terms of striving to maintain accessibility in the public right-of-way..."
- **[[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-24|1-ER-24]]** — the colloquy continues; the court presses: "you don't want to sanction people sleeping on the streets, you just want to enforce any — I mean, **you won't prevent them from sleeping on the streets?**" Counsel calls the sanction/enforcement line "a significant distinction" — conceding non-prevention.
- **[[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-38|1-ER-38]], [[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-42|1-ER-42]], [[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-51|1-ER-51]]** — argument that displaced residents may go to "areas the City said people have the legal right to be" in "a city with no camping ban" ([[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-42|1-ER-42]]); "a place that they already, **as the City has said**, have a legal right to be" ([[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-38|1-ER-38]]).

## IV. The City administers habitation in practice (program conduct)

- **[[ER 0637-0658 - Declaration of Thomas Gregory re Completion of ADA Interactive Process…#ER-639|4-ER-639]] ¶ 7** and **[[ER 1261-1279 - Declaration of Thomas Gregory ISO of City of Berkeley's Motion for Sum…#ER-1262|6-ER-1262]] ¶ 7** — ADA Coordinator Thomas Gregory, both declarations: "I am familiar with the City's **encampment management and abatement program, sidewalk management program**, shelter and housing programs, parking enforcement program, and related operational practices." The City's own ADA officer names these as City programs he administers accommodation requests within.
- **[[ER 0179-0229 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Motion for Class Certificat…#ER-213|2-ER-213]]** — Class cert order (Prado, Dkt. 261), quoting the BHU SJ order: the City's encampment management program "**does not operate by categorizing locations as universally permissible or impermissible — it operates by assessing conditions on the ground** and responding to hazardous or obstructive conditions as they arise." Habitation persists unless conditions trigger intervention.
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-105|1-ER-105]]–106** — City position as recounted in the SJ order: the encampment management program is "designed to enable prompt intervention **when encampments pose imminent risks** to public health and safety" — i.e., absent such risks, encampments remain under the program.
- **[[ER 1102-1105 - Opposition to Ex Parte Application for Emergency Hearing#ER-1104|6-ER-1104]]–1105** — City opposition (Dkt. 249): ADA coordinator "stopped at **every tent and RV** and spoke with anyone he saw **on the street or sidewalk**... and asked whether they had a dis[ability]"; "the City conducted extensive in person outreach to help identify and potentially accommodate the needs of disabled residents **in the Harrison Street encampment**." The City runs its ADA interactive process *to* people living on the street, about living on the street.
- **[[ER 1907-1911 - Order Granting Motion for TRO#ER-1908|10-ER-1908]]** — TRO order (2/14/25, Dkt. 18): the City "appears to acknowledge" that "compliance with municipal code enforcement can constitute a benefit of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity under Title II" (*McGary*); plaintiffs' requests were "an opportunity to **stay at 8th and Harrison Streets** rather than relocate."
- **[[ER 1328-1351 - Notice of Motion and Motion to Dissolve Preliminary Injunction#ER-1341|7-ER-1341]]** and **[[ER 1628-1631 - Declaration of Adam Weisberg ISO of Parties’ Joint Status Update#ER-1631|8-ER-1631]]** — "The encampment on Harrison Street **has existed for a long time**" (City's own motion papers / supporting declaration) — years of habitation under the City's management.
- **[[ER 0845-0857 - Opposition to Motion for TRO#ER-846|4-ER-846]]** — City opposition: notice of intent to abate first posted **January 2025**; the encampment remained (partly under injunction) into 2026 — a year-plus of administered, tolerated habitation.
- **[[ER 0969-1006 - Exhibit B – Fourth Posting Public Notice#ER-977|5-ER-977]]–996** and **[[ER 1106-1143 - Exhibit A – Abatement Public Notice and Information#ER-1114|6-ER-1114]]–1171** — City public notices and resource packets addressed to "**the people living in and around the Harrison Street encampment**," including "Leptospirosis Resources for Encampments near Harrison Street" — the City publishing health guidance *for* street residents as such. [OCR on some pages]
- **[[ER 0230-0275 - Supplemental Declaration of Anthony Prince ISO of Plaintiff’s Reply to…#ER-273|2-ER-273]]** — Prince Supp. Decl.: history of City-sanctioned habitation — in April 2021 the City responded to the Mayor's and Council's "sanctioned encampment" proposal by **leasing a lot on Grayson Street that "hosted tents inside the warehouse and RVs on the parking lot"** (program ran until Dec. 2022). [OCR]
- **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-97|1-ER-97]]** — SJ order intro: describes the "encampment of unhoused residents located around Harrison Street & 8th Street" as the subject of the City's abatement "efforts" — the program's object is an inhabited street.

## Counter-arguments to anticipate (know the record both ways)

- The City repeatedly disclaims maintaining "sanctioned encampment areas" or "enforcement-free zones": **[[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-108|1-ER-108]]**, **[[ER 0483-0491 - Exhibit H – Email Thread for Jonny Ruel Holder#ER-489|3-ER-489]]–490**, **[[ER 0384-0400 - Defendant City of Berkeley’s Opposition Ex Parte Motion for TRO and Or…#ER-389|2-ER-389]], [[ER 0384-0400 - Defendant City of Berkeley’s Opposition Ex Parte Motion for TRO and Or…#ER-392|2-ER-392]]**, **[[ER 0637-0658 - Declaration of Thomas Gregory re Completion of ADA Interactive Process…#ER-641|4-ER-641]]–642**. Response: the program definition adopted at [[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-124|1-ER-124]]–125 does not depend on "sanctioning" — it rests on the *actual long-standing use* of streets as abode and the City's condition-based management of that use.
- **[[ER 1380-1423 - Joint Status Report#ER-1388|7-ER-1388]]** describes sidewalk-use rules as ensuring use "remains **temporary**." Response: temporariness goes to the program's *rules*, not its *scope* — the same sentence admits the program regulates individuals' sidewalk use with structures.
- **[[ER 0002-0080 - Transcript of Proceedings Held March 20, 2026 BHU#ER-24|1-ER-24]]** — City counsel distinguishes "not conducting enforcement" from "sanctioning." The court itself called this "kind of a fine line," and the program definition it later adopted ([[ER 0097-0170 - Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Cross- Motions for Summary…#ER-124|1-ER-124]]) mooted the distinction.
