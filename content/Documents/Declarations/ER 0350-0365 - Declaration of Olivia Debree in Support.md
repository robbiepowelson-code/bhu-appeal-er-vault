DECLARATION OF OLVIA DEBREE
I, Olivia deBree, declare:
1. I have personal knowledge of the facts set forth in this declaration.
2. I am a licensed and board-certified Nurse Practitioner in the state of California. My
credentials are AGPCNP. I have provided medical care to unhoused patients through
LifeLong Medical Care for 10 years, including for the last 6 years on the streets of West
Berkeley.
3. I am the medical provider for the following members of the Berkeley Homeless Union,
whom I have treated since the dates indicated: Eric Keiser (since 5/29/2020), Shareef
Muwwakkil (since 10/5/2020), Lewanda Parnell (since 11/8/2022), Erin Spencer (since
5/1/2020), and Austin White (since 9/24/2024).
Verification of Medical Conditions
4. The diagnoses, functional limitations, and medical necessity of accommodations described
herein are documented in the medical verification letters I have authored for each patient,
submitted to the City of Berkeley as part of their reasonable accommodation requests. I
incorporate by reference those letters, attached here to this declaration as Exhibit A, which
provide detailed clinical support for the opinions discussed.
General Medical Opinion on Stability
5. For patients with severe chronic physical and mental health disabilities, stable housing and a
predictable environment are not merely beneficial—they are medically necessary treatment
modalities. The trauma of suddenly losing one’s home, the physical demands of constant
relocation, and the loss of community support associated with displacement cause acute
health crises and increase mortality in unhoused patients.

Patient-Specific Diagnoses and Risks of Forced Displacement:
7. Homelessness has created very unfavorable conditions for my patients’ health. Abatements in
which my patients are forced to move elsewhere on the street temporarily or permanently
exacerbate many health conditions.
8. Eric Keiser: Diagnosed with chronic leg pain from an acquired deformity of his left lower leg
after a traumatic fracture, abnormal gait, and schizophrenia with disorganized thinking and
auditory hallucinations. His mobility and communication abilities are limited, and his
executive functioning is impaired. A forced move could: (1) exacerbate chronic pain and risk
re-injury from falls; (2) escalate psychotic symptoms and hallucinations due to trauma.
9. Shareef Muwakkil: Diagnosed with chronic leg swelling, delayed healing and malunion of a
fractured left distal fibula, 2 myocardial infarctions in 2024, and a severe mood disorder with
psychotic features. He cannot stand/walk long distances or lift items safely. A sweep would:
(1) risk cardiac strain and physical injury; (2) likely overwhelm his executive functioning,
and could cause or intensify psychosis and depressive symptoms; (3) potentially trigger
patient to withdraw from social interactions, hampering his ability to navigate housing 
systems.
10. Lewanda Parnell: Diagnosed with osteoarthritic knees and urinary incontinence. She uses a
stroller as a mobility device to prevent falls and relies on proximity to services and her
community for support in completing daily tasks and stability. Displacement could: (1)
increase her fall risk and risk of injury; (2) make her especially vulnerable in terms of safety

11. Erin Spencer: Diagnosed with chronic musculoskeletal pain and range of motion issues
(clavicle, low back with sciatica), PTSD, and history of depression with psychiatric hospital
admissions. Stress impairs his executive function. A forced relocation would: (1) risk
intensified pain and worsened injury; (2) potentially trigger PTSD crises, panic, and decision

12. Austin White: Diagnosed with esophageal varices (bulging vessels in the esophagus that can
rupture and hemorrhage), bipolar disorder, alcoholic cirrhosis, and peripheral neuropathy. He
is medically restricted from lifting >20 pounds. His service dogs are essential for emotional
stability. Displacement could: (1) increase the risk of a esophageal bleed from physical
strain; (2) cause severe emotional destabilization; (3) overwhelm his executive functioning,
impairing his ability to complete daily tasks for survival.
Conclusion
13. Based on my clinical expertise, the permanent displacement of these patients from the
Harrison Street encampment without the offer of either a sanctioned encampment site or an
indoor transitional or permanent housing option carries potentially grave, immediate, and
irreparable consequences for their health and safety. It could directly cause: hypertensive
emergencies, cardiac strain, physical injuries, psychotic breaks, suicidal and homicidal
ideation, and the loss of medically necessary equipment and community care. The harm
could be severe, irreversible, life-threatening, and satisfy the legal standard for irreparable
harm.
