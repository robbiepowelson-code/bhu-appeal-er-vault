DECLARATION OF YESICA PRADO
I, Yesica Prado, declare:
1. I have personal knowledge of the facts set forth in this declaration. If called as a witness, I 
could and would testify competently thereto.
2. I am a witness to this case. I am the President of the Berkeley Homeless Union. I have been 
residing at 8th and Harrison streets encampment since August 2018.
3. On March 10, 2026, at 11:01 a.m., I sent an email to City Attorneys Chris Jensen and Nubyaan 
Scott—counsel representing the City in this matter—notifying them that BHU would be refiling its ex parte motion for a temporary restraining order today.
4. I submit this supplemental declaration in direct response to the Court's March 9, 2026 Order, 
which found that my prior declaration did not constitute an adequate evidentiary basis to 
evaluate Plaintiffs' ADA claims. This declaration provides the concrete evidence the Court 
requested, including:
a. Proof of standing for each individual on whose behalf this motion is brought;
b. Documentation of the City's unlawful denials of otherwise meritorious 
accommodation requests;
c. Specific evidence of the City's failure to engage in the interactive process; and
d. A complete exhibit list organizing all supporting documents.
5. The Court required BHU to "establish standing on behalf of any such individuals." To address 
this requirement, I have attached signed BHU Accommodation Request Forms for each 
individual named in this motion.
6. Attached as Exhibit B are true and correct copies of the signed BHU Accommodation Request 
Forms for the following individuals, each authorizing BHU to represent them in the 
accommodation process and in this litigation.
7. Each of these individuals, except Jacob Clakley who has been taken to a medical respite hotel,
remains at the Harrison Street encampment as of March 10, 2026, and faces imminent 
enforcement the next day at 6:00 a.m. on March 11.

Evidence of the City's Failure to Engage in the Interactive Process
28. To demonstrate the City's failure to engage in a good faith interactive process, I note the 
following:
a. For nearly all individuals, the "interactive process" consisted solely of BHU 
submitting an email request and the City responding with a "final determination."
b. There were no meetings. There was no back-and-forth dialogue. There was no 
exploration of alternatives, despite BHU’s request for each individual. 
29. On March 7, 2026, this Court issued an Order relying on the City's representation that it "is 
engaged in the interactive process with those individuals and will not forcibly remove any of 
them until that process is complete."
30. This representation was false. By March 5, 2026—two days before the Court issued its 
Order—the City had already begun issuing "final determinations" declaring the process over 
for multiple individuals. The City never corrected the record.

31. For nearly a year, Thomas Gregory alone issued "final determinations" denying 
accommodation requests with “fundamental alteration” and “undue burden” claims—despite 
lacking authority to do so. I read online that under 28 C.F.R. § 35.164, such determinations 
must be made by "the head of the public entity or his or her designee."
32. In January 2026, after BHU repeatedly requested compliance with this regulation, City 
Manager Paul Buddenhagen suddenly began signing "final determinations." This was an 
attempt to create the illusion of compliance.
33. But now, Thomas Gregory is once more issuing "final determinations" with “fundamental 
alteration” and “undue burden” claims. This shell game demonstrates bad faith.
34. I respectfully request that the Court grant the requested temporary restraining order to prevent 
irreparable harm to BHU members with denied accommodation requests before enforcement 
begins at 6:00 a.m. on March 11, 2026.
My Attempt to Obtain Clarification from the City Was Ignored
35. On March 9, with enforcement less than 24 hours away according to the public notice, our 
members remained confused and frightened about their status. Despite the City posting notices 
four separate times, no outreach worker had ever come to explain them. Campers had explicitly 
asked for clarification including myself—and received none. The notices remained 
unexplained and inaccessible.
36. To address this confusion and to seek the clarity our members deserved, I sent an email on that 
day to ADA Coordinator Thomas Gregory, with copies to City Manager Paul Buddenhagen 
and the City Attorney's Office.

a. Whether the March 10 extension applied to all individuals or only some;
b. What the extension actually meant—whether it protected only persons, or also their 
belongings, tents, RVs, vans, and support animals;
c. Whether the interactive process was truly complete for those who received "final 
determinations";
