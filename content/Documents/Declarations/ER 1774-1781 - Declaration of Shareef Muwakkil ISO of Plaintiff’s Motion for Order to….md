approximately eight years. I’m a member of the Berkeley Homeless Union. I have been 
residing at the 8th and Harrison Streets encampment for 3 ½ years. 
2. My disabilities are physical and mental. I have experienced chronic swelling in both legs 
for the past three years, resulting in mobility limitations from day to day. I also have a 
heart valve condition that limits his ability to lift, carry, or move personal belongings

On July 18, I attended an in-person meeting with City of Berkeley officials to discuss 
these accommodations. Present were Thomas Gregory, the City’s ADA Coordinator; Iris Mattes, 
a representative from the City Attorney’s office; Olivia DeBree from LifeLong Medical; 
members of the Berkeley Homeless Union, including Yesica Prado and Gordon Gilmore, and 
officer of the California Homeless Union Robbie Powelson. A video recording was initiated to 
document the interactive process should the court need to review it.
6. The meeting began with Mr. Gregory introducing himself and handing me his business 
card, stating that we had not met before. Ms. Mattes introduced herself next, then abruptly cut off 
further introductions and shifted into questioning me about the legitimacy of my ADA request 
form and the union's involvement in my request. After I confirmed her questions and I was 
reviewing the email communications on my hands, she abruptly changed tone and stated she did 
not want any threats made to her, her family, or any city staff and demanded that I personally 
reassure her that I would not make any threats.

7. This comment came to me as a complete shock. We were only two minutes into the 
meeting. I had made no such threats nor did I conduct myself in any disrespectful way. I calmly 
told Ms. Mattes: “I find that inflammatory. You don't know me. I mean, when we just started this 
thing, I don't even know who you are.” I asked her if she had made the same demand to any 
other encampment resident before and she said, “No.”
8. Still, Ms. Mattes continued, demanding that I and every individual in the room verbally 
agreed not to threaten her or her colleague or else the meeting could not continue. I was 
extremely disturbed by this accusatory framing and stated: “I don't normally engage with people 
like that and I do find it insulting but I was willing to let that go in order for us to move 
forward.” Despite our repeated attempts to move the meeting forward in good faith, Ms. Mattes 
returned to her demand multiple times, stating that she needed “assurance” and repeating her 
concern about verbal threats. 
9. Others present stepped in to voice concern. Olivia, speaking as a medical professional, 
warned that the confrontational tone could trigger a mental health crisis and cause me to 
decompensate. She explained that I live with PTSD and other mental health conditions, and that 
Ms. Mattes’s aggressive approach was not only inappropriate, but clinically dangerous. 
10. Robbie emphasized that no one in the room disagreed with the basic ground rule against 
threats, saying, “Everyone agrees with it.” Despite these clear warnings and unanimous 
agreement, Ms. Mattes kept pushing the issue in a hostile and controlling manner, attempting to 
force everyone in the room to bow down to her demands, like we were the problem. It felt more 
like bullying than setting mutual rules for an effective meeting. I asked how many meetings were 
started in this manner and they responded none, so I asked not to do it at this meeting.

11. Eventually, Ms. Mattes stepped out to consult with her supervisor and returned saying the 
supervisor was unavailable. She then stated that she had not received confirmation from all 
attendees of her requested ground rule but would proceed under the condition that if a threat 
were made, she would end the meeting. No threats were ever made.
12. After about 15 minutes of what felt like a rude and hostile start, Mr. Gregory was able to 
begin the meeting. He stated that the City does not consider shelter to be a reasonable 
accommodation under the ADA, and referred us to the Homeless Response Team (HRT) for 
information about shelter availability. In response, BHU officer Yesica Prado stated that there 
were no beds right now, and asked: “If you don't have shelter, where do you want Mr. 
Muwakkill to relocate that won't exacerbate his health conditions?” Mr. Gregory responded 
plainly: “I don’t know.”
13. At one point, Mr. Gregory framed my request as wanting to stay "indefinitely" at the 
encampment, but I am not trying to stay "indefinitely" on the streets. I want to get back into 
housing as soon as possible. Still as an alternative, Mr. Gregory only offered me an extension of 
72 hours to pack and leave the encampment. He said: “I feel comfortable giving 72 hours to 
anyone with a disability being asked to vacate. I feel comfortable with that.”
14. I pointed out: “on the surface that sounds reasonable. My question would be to where?” 
Mr. Gregory replied: “I understand that huge dilemma for campers that they don't have a place to 
go. I understand that. And I understand that problem. That is not a problem that I can solve via 
an ADA.”

15. When asked if the City had any safe locations where I could relocate, we were told 
repeatedly that no such location exists. The Union representatives then reminded Mr. Gregory 
and Ms. Mattes that on June 10, 2025, Ms. Mattes told Judge Chen in Court that there were other 
locations at which people cleared from 8th and Harrison could camp, but neither Mr. Gregory 
nor Ms. Mattes would agree to identify these areas. I told them, “Then I can’t make an informed 
decision about whether 72 hours is enough time. That doesn’t make any sense.”
16. Mr. Gregory responded by asking me to tell him how much time I needed, and suggested 
100 hours, but again, without a destination, I am not able to calculate the amount of time. I 
reiterated that I needed to evaluate my situation, assess my belongings, and figure out a plan, 
none of which can happen overnight or without some known alternative.
20. At that point, my clinician Olivia spoke up because she was genuinely concerned about 
what this was doing to me. She explained to the group that I’ve had two heart attacks since 
January 2024 and that I live with congestive heart failure, PTSD, and mobility issues from an old 
ankle injury that never healed right. She made it clear that the City’s ongoing push to displace 
me isn’t just stressful, it could actually put my life at risk. 
21. Olivia talked about how being forced to move again could trigger a health crisis, both 
physically and mentally, and how all of this fuels a deep sense of hopelessness and desperation. 
Most importantly, she warned that I could lose the only medical access I currently have. Mr. 
Gregory then admitted that if I was displaced with no safe place to go my connection to medical 
services could be severed.
