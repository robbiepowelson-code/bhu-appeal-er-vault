3. Previously, I had been living in a smaller vehicle where, when I was sleeping, my feet
couldn’t be level. This caused my legs and my feet to swell painfully. I still own this vehicle too. It runs

4. I identify as someone with disabilities. My physical disabilities are severe scoliosis and

5. Living in my van makes me much safer than I was. Living in a tent means being
vulnerable to rats and also people that are dangerous. My van keeps me warm and dry and my back is 
doing so much better. 
6. I was completely unaware that the City was planning to close the encampment where I
live until an attorney from East Bay Community Law Center, Brigitte Nicoletti, and her intern Nina 
Liss-Schultz, came by on February 20, 2026, at about 2pm to check in on me. She asked me if I was 
aware that the City was planning to close the encampment next week, and I had no idea. 
7. Ms. Nicoletti showed me a copy of the notice of closure for February 24, 2026. The
Notice is had the subject line: “Notice of Encampment Closure,” and stated it had been posted February 
17, 2026. I had never seen this notice before. The City definitely did not post it on or near my vehicle in 
the last few days and no one from the City has been by to speak with me. I have been in my vehicle and 
would have seen them had they come by. I also have not seen anything like the Notice posted on my 
block. There are also no “No Parking” signs that have been put up on my block for February 24, 2026. 
8. I read the list of people that the Notice states it applies to. My vehicles are parked on one
of the streets within the geographic area of the closure and I am not one of the people listed as protected 
from the closure. Ms. Nicoletti also showed me “Attachment 3” in the Notice which shows my street as 
part of the encampment red zone. As a result, I believe that the Notice of Closure applies to me and my 
belongings and vehicles.
