ability to move and walk long distances, lift heavy items and access services without help.

3. From the start, the tone of the meeting felt adversarial. City staff began by questioning 
whether BHU had properly submitted the ADA request for me, rather than asking me directly 
about my needs. It felt like they were trying to go around the process instead of offering help.
4. I asked for reasonable accommodations, including: (1) for the City to replace my 
destroyed tent, which I customized to meet my needs; (2) to temporarily remain in my current 
location due to my disabilities and the support system I rely on there; (3) temporary pause on 
enforcement to be protected from being displaced again; (4) for a stable safe place to relocate 
that doesn’t make my health condition worse; and (5) for help packing and retrieving my 
property if I’m forced to move.
5. The City denied or deflected nearly every request. They said my tent was not an ADA 
accommodation, even though it protects me from physical and mental health triggers. They 
claimed they had no obligation to provide tents or help me replace what they destroyed, even 
though they slashed my tent and gave me no notice. 
6. Mr. Gregory also claimed my request to remain in place would “fundamentally alter” 
their enforcement policy but never explained to me how or offered an alternative site that would 
be safe and accessible for me.
7. When I asked how they expect me to find a new location in just a few days, especially 
with my disabilities and no transportation, they had no answer. I said again and again: “I’ve got
