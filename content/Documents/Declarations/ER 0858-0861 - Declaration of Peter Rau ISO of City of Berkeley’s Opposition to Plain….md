19. Based on my observations and my experience working on rodent abatement and vector 
control issues, vehicles that remain stationary for long periods can create conditions that support 
rodent harborage. Rodents commonly use sheltered spaces such as the areas beneath vehicles as 
locations to travel, nest, and access food sources. When vehicles remain unmoved, those areas 
can become protected environments where rodent burrows, nesting material, and other signs of 
rodent activity develops. 
20. Stationary vehicles can also prevent inspection and treatment of the ground surfaces 
beneath them. Effective rodent abatement requires the ability to inspect for burrows, place traps, 
and treat areas including the use of rodenticide where rodents are nesting or traveling. When 
vehicles, especially those that are used as shelter, remain in the same location for extended 
periods of time, they can obstruct access to those areas and prevent the safe use of rodenticide, 
and make it more difficult for vector control personnel to identify and address rodent harborage. 
21. For these reasons, moving stationary vehicles, including RVs, can be necessary to allow 
inspection of the underlying ground surfaces and to implement effective rodent abatement 
measures.
