JUDGMENT BRIEFING SCHEDULE
The Court has reviewed the parties’ Joint Status Report (Dkts. 194, 200) regarding 
completion of the interactive process proceedings. The parties continue to dispute the 
reasonableness and feasibility of certain accommodations requested by Plaintiffs and the adequacy 
of the City’s explanations for denying them. The Court directs the parties to file cross-motions for 
summary judgment. The motions shall address, inter alia, the following matters:
1. The City is directed to explain why providing limited moving assistance, portable 
facilities, or trash disposal services during an interim period of relocation would 
fundamentally alter the City’s encampment response program or impose an undue 
administrative burden. Discuss any alternative accommodations that were considered in 
order to provide reasonable assistance, and on what grounds they were rejected. 
With respect to urgent public health concerns raised by the City in connection with 
providing relocation assistance, the City shall explain why those risks cannot be mitigated 
through personal protective equipment or other measures. Plaintiffs shall explain the 
disability-related basis for their requested accommodation, and any reasonable alternatives
that may be available to them.

3. The City is directed to explain why providing non-congregate, ADA-compliant relocation

accessible shelter and housing options, in light of their disabilities.

fundamentally alter the City’s sidewalk management program. The City shall provide 
factual information about sidewalk width and pedestrian egress, and identify any 
alternatives considered, such as modified placement or reasonable dimensions that are 
feasible, and the reasons they were rejected. Plaintiffs are directed to explain the specific 
nexus between any alleged disabilities and the requested accommodations for increased 
tent dimensions or sidewalk space. Plaintiffs shall also identify any reasonable 
alternatives. 
5. The City is directed to describe current on the ground conditions with respect to RV 
parking and roadway functioning, and explain why an accommodation exempting certain 
vehicles from time-limited parking enforcement fundamentally alters the City’s parking or 
roadway programs or imposes an undue administrative burden. The City shall describe 
alternative measures that were considered, such as temporary permits or designated 
parking zones, and why they were deemed infeasible or inadequate. Plaintiffs are directed 
to explain the nexus between any disabilities and the alleged inability to comply with
