NOTICE OF MOTION AND MOTION TO DISSOLVE PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION 
TO ALL PARTIES AND THEIR COUNSEL OF RECORD:
PLEASE TAKE NOTICE that on March 5, 2026, at 1:30 p.m., in Courtroom #5, 17th 
Floor, or at any other time as the Court may order, Defendant CITY OF BERKELEY (“City”), 
will move this Court, pursuant to the Court’s inherent equitable authority, for an order dissolving 
the preliminary injunction issued on June 4, 2025 and serially extended on August 5, 2025, 
September 12, 2025, October 23, 2025, and January 13, 2026. Dkt. Nos. 104, 131, 140, 141, 
162, and 202. 
Defendant makes this motion on the grounds that (1) the sole condition precedent to 
lifting the injunction—completion of the ADA interactive process with specific individuals—has 
been met by the City, (2) the Court materially altered the preliminary injunction on January 13, 
2026 without notice contrary to the requirements of Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(a)(1) by holding that the 
interactive process is not ended until the Court determines each individuals’ underlying ADA 
claims, (3) new facts since the injunction was issued materially shift the Winter factors in favor 
of the City and the public, and (4) the length of the injunction to date exceeds the Court’s 
authority to prohibit enforcement of City ordinance related to environmental health and safety 
and sidewalk usage. This motion is based upon this Notice of Motion, the below Memorandum 
of Points and Authorities, the Complaint and other papers on file in this action, and other matters 
as may be presented to the Court at or before the time of the hearing.

MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The sole condition imposed on Defendant City of Berkeley (“City”) in the limited 
injunction that the Court issued on June 10, 2025 —to complete the ADA interactive process 
with certain individuals residing in the Harrison Corridor encampment—has been met. The City 
has worked diligently and in good faith to complete all required interactive process discussions 
despite the best efforts of Plaintiff Berkeley Homeless Union (“BHU”), its members, and its 
attorney to delay and obstruct those discussions. Based on this alone, dissolution of the 
preliminary injunction would be warranted. However, new factual circumstances and the sheer 
length of time the injunction has been in place also demand the same result. For the reasons 
discussed below, the Court should dissolve the preliminary injunction originally issued on June 
10, 2025 and lift the prohibition against requiring those named individuals encamped in the 
Harrison Corridor to vacate the area. 
II. PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND FACTUAL BACKGROUND
 The procedural history and factual basis related this lawsuit has been set forth in 
numerous prior filings and the City hereby incorporates them as if fully set forth within. See, 
e.g., Dkt. Nos. 56, 82, 107, 148, 155, and 194. The City provides a brief summary of the relevant 
factual and procedural details below.

ADA.

only on its claims of state-created danger under the Fourteenth Amendment and for the City’s 
alleged failure to engage in the interactive process required by the ADA. [[ER 1898-1905 - Notice of and Motion for Preliminary Injunction to Enjoin Displacement…|Dkt. No. 72]] at 4-5.

A. BHU Failed to Perform As Ordered By the Court. 
The June 10, 2025 Order was not just an injunction against the City. It was a mandate to 
both parties to “complete the interactive process” for BHU members who had pending requests 
at the time of the Court’s order. [[ER 1863-1865 - Civil Minutes|Dkt. No. 104]] at 3. But BHU failed to engage in the interactive 
process as required by the ADA, meaning BHU has failed to comply with the injunction order. 
The ADA requires public entities to engage in good faith with qualified individuals with 
disabilities, consider reasonable modifications, and avoid discriminatory exclusions from 
services or programs. See 42 U.S.C. § 12132; 28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7). Courts have emphasized 
that the interactive process is a mutual obligation, requiring cooperation from both the public 
entity and the individual seeking accommodations. See Beck v. Univ. of Wisconsin Bd. of 
Regents, 75 F.3d 1130, 1135 (7th Cir. 1996). Good faith does not allow for neglected scheduling 
obligations, infrequent or nonexistent attendance, and obstructive methods. It also requires clear 
communication, meaningful dialogue, and a willingness to accept reasonable modifications of 
policy that are within the boundaries of what the law allows and what is feasible for the entity. 
On the other hand, “[a] party that obstructs or delays the interactive process is not acting in good 
faith. A party that fails to communicate, by way of initiation or response, may also be acting in 
bad faith.” Humphrey v. Mem’l Hosps. Ass’n, 239 F.3d 1128, 1137 (9th Cir. 2001) (citing Beck, 
75 F.3d at 1135 (7th Cir.1996)). The facts set forth above demonstrate BHU’s extreme and 
unreasonable delay tactics—including insistence on in-person meetings even after the Court 
ruled otherwise, last minute cancelations, attempts to involve extraneous participants such as the

E. The Length of the Injunction to Date Exceeds the Court’s Authority under the 
ADA to Prohibit Enforcement of City Ordinances Related to Environmental 
Health and Safety and Sidewalk Usage. 
In this case, the preliminary injunction was issued to allow time to conduct the 
interactive process for requested accommodations under the ADA. However, the length of the 
injunction clearly runs afoul of the Ninth Circuit’s decision in Caltrans, 32 F.4th 852. Under 
Caltrans, Title II of the ADA does not empower a district court to issue a six-month long 
injunction forcing the delay of a public-safety encampment abatement. Id. at 862. As the Ninth

reasonable accommodation under the ADA into a de facto entitlement to remain on public

of way in the Harrison Corridor. 
In Caltrans, due in part to the agency’s determination of a critical need to clear the 
encampment in question, the Ninth Circuit vacated an injunction that required Caltrans to delay 
clearing an encampment for six months to allow the parties to engage in the interactive process. 
32 F.4th at 865. The court held that such a delay was itself a prohibited fundamental alteration 
under Title II because it impermissibly minimized the public safety purpose of the clearance and 
ignored how the balance of the hardships shifted over time in favor of closure. Id. at 864-65. The 
same is true here. This Court’s injunction has contravened Caltrans by minimizing the safety 
risks that make “clearing [priority] encampments so critical,” and by ignoring how these safety 
risks have grown over time. Id. at 862. This goes against this Court’s own recognition that “[a]s 
an encampment continues over time and if health and safety hazards persist, the government’s 
interest in closure increases.” [[ER 1863-1865 - Civil Minutes|Dkt. No. 104]] at 1. 
At the time the preliminary injunction issued, the City already had documented years of 
public health and safety risks posed by the encampment including exposed sewage, scattered 
bloodied needles, unmitigated human and animal waste, rotting food, extensive rodent burrows,
