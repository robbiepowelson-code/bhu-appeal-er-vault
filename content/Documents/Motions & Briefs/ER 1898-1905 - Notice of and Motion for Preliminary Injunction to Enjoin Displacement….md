This motion seeks to enjoin the City of Berkeley from displacing or removing unhoused members of 
the Berkeley Homeless Union currently residing at the Eighth and Harrison encampment until the 
City has engaged in a good faith interactive process pursuant to Title II of the Americans with 
Disabilities Act and applicable federal regulations.
This Motion is based on this Notice, the accompanying Memorandum of Points and Authorities, 
supporting Declarations, the concurrently filed Motion to Shorten Time pursuant to Civil Local Rule 
6-3, all other records on file in this matter, and any evidence or argument presented at the hearing on
this motion.

interactive process with only two other BHU members, Adrien Bouchard and Nicholas Johnson.

STANDARD OF REVIEW
Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 65, the Court has the authority to issue a preliminary 
injunction. A party seeking such preliminary relief must meet one of two variants of the same 
standard. The traditional Winter standard requires the movant to show that (1) it “is likely to succeed 
on the merits;” (2) it “is likely to suffer irreparable harm in the absence of preliminary relief;” (3) 
“the balance of equities tips in [its] favor;” and (4) “an injunction is in the public interest.” Winter v. 
Natural Res. Def. Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7, 20 (2008). Under the “sliding scale” variant of the same 
standard, “if a plaintiff can only show that there are ‘serious questions going to the merits’—a lesser 
showing than likelihood of success on the merits—then a preliminary injunction may still issue if the 
‘balance of hardships tips sharply in the plaintiff’s favor,’ and the other two Winter factors are 
satisfied.” All. for the Wild Rockies v. Peña, 865 F.3d 1211, 1217 (9th Cir. 2017) (emphasis in 
original) (quoting Shell Offshore, Inc. v. Greenpeace, Inc., 709 F.3d 1281, 1291 (9th Cir. 2013)).

SERIOUS QUESTIONS GOING TO THE MERITS
State Created Danger Doctrine – Fourteenth Amendment
The Ninth Circuit recognizes a substantive due process violation under the Fourteenth 
Amendment where a state actor “affirmatively place[s] an individual in danger by acting with deliberate 
indifference to [a] known or obvious danger in subjecting the plaintiff to it.” (citation). Deliberate 
indifference exists where the defendant “disregard[s] a known or obvious consequence of [its] action.” 
(citation).” Boyd v City of San Rafael 3:23-cv-04085-EMC N.D. Oct. 19 2023
First, since the initial TRO, The City of Berkeley has refused to meet with BHU members 
and officers to engage in ADA interactive assessments. Mr. Gregory made an arbitrary decision to 
require medical verification before meeting - even when BHU members have obvious visible 
disabilities such as walking with crutches [Decl Prado¶1-5]. BHU Officer Prado had informed Mr. 
Gregory that was impossible because of BHU members limited access to medical services, and the 
fact their primary health provider through Live Long Medical Care has been out of office - making it 
literally impossible to provide the requested documents Decl Prado ¶6. In Spite of this, on May 12th 
Mr. Gregory indicated the city will clear the camp if the TRO is extinguished. Id. Accordingly, the 
City has deliberately disregarded BHU members health and safety and need for accommodations for 
the planned sweep by being dilatory in in meeting with BHU members. 
This poses an imminent harm to Plaintiffs, because BHU members rely on the close 
proximity of the portable bathrooms at Eighth and Harrison, food and water, mutual protection, and 
loss of regular medical care that regularly comes to the encampment Prado ¶7. While these 
foreseeable harms could be mitigated through an interactive process, the City has made no such 
effort. Instead, the City is permanently shutting down public spaces across the City – such as Ohlone 
Park See ECF 68, 68-7. 68-13. Accordingly, BHU members are fighting to survive to maintain

Title II Americans With Disability Act
Title II of the ADA provides that “no qualified individual with a disability shall, by reason of 
such disability, be excluded from participation in or be denied the benefits of the services, programs, 
or activities of a public entity, or be subjected to discrimination by any such entity.” 42 U.S.C. § 
12132. Its implementing regulations require state agencies to “make reasonable modifications in 
policies, practices, or procedures when the modifications are necessary to avoid discrimination on 
the basis of disability, unless the public entity can demonstrate that making the modifications would 
fundamentally alter the nature of the service, program, or activity.” 28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7)(i).
Quoting from Boyd v City of San Rafael 3:23-cv-04085-EMC N.D. Oct. 19 2023. “The refusal to 
engage in the accommodation process may constitute a denial of a request”Boyd v City of San Rafael 
3:23-cv-04085-EMC N.D. Oct. 19 2023. Id. 
Here, the City of Berkeley has constructively refused to engage in the interactive process for 
19 BHU members by refusing to meet and engage the interactive process without medical 
information they have been informed cannot be quickly obtain. Decl Prado ¶5. In Boyd , the court 
issued an injunction in which not medical verification was provided – which is to be expected 
because unhoused people experience extreme, dire poverty that alienates them from most medical 
systems. Accordingly, the City’s unreasonable barriers to the ADA accommodation process for 19 
BHU members constitutes unreasonable denials of ADA requests.

Irreparable Harm 
Irreparable harm is traditionally defined as harm for which there is no adequate legal remedy, 
such as an award of damages. Ariz. Dream Act Coal. v. Brewer, 757 F.3d 1053, 1068 (9th Cir. 
2014). The harm must be likely to occur not merely possible. Winter, 555 U.S. at 22. Where, as here, 
the threat alleged is sufficiently serious the requisite likelihood of harm is lowered. See, e.g., Roman 
v. Wolf, 977 F.3d 935, 944 (9th Cir. 2020) (finding likelihood of irreparable harm based on risk of
death from COVID-19 which was approximately one percent at that time according to the Center for 
Disease Control and Prevention). Quoting from Boyd v City of San Rafael 3:23-cv-04085 N.D. Oct
