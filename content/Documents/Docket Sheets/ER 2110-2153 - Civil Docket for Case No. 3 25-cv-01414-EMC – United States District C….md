The Court rules that the interactive process required under the Americans with
Disabilities Act does not need to occur in person. The interactive process may be
conducted in person, via Zoom or other videoconferencing platforms, by telephone,
or through any other live process that allows meaningful real-time two-way
communication. See Scwarz v. Town of Fairfax, No. 25-cv-06395-RS, 2025 WL
2410522, at *4 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 19, 2025) (permitting interactive process to proceed
via Zoom). The Court rejects the City's request to conduct the interactive process
exclusively through written email exchanges. The parties are reminded of their
continuing obligation to engage in good faith efforts to complete the interactive
process and to work cooperatively toward reasonable accommodations that address
BHU members disability-related needs.
With respect to BHU's motion regarding the scope of the preliminary injunction 140
, the Court finds that BHU has not provided admissible evidence demonstrating that
any additional persons resided in the Harrison Corridor as of June 10, 2025. The
Prado Declaration lacks personal knowledge of the individuals' locations and is
contradicted by the City's census data. Accordingly, the preliminary injunction
applies only to the individuals that the City has identified and conceded are covered.
Mr. Prince's request for an extension of time to file a status report on the interactive
process is denied without prejudice. The Court may reconsider the request upon
submission of a doctor's note or other documentation substantiating the need for
additional time.
