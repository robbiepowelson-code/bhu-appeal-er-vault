Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) resulting from his military service in the Gulf War (Kuwait, 1993–1997),

manner that does not exacerbate his documented disabilities:

Due to his PTSD and need for daily structure, Mr. Coon requires either:
A non-congregate ADA-compliant shelter space, or
A clearly designated, safe location where he may remain without harassment or risk of sudden displacement,
particularly during City operations.

services — even during times when he was present and visible at his location.

Sirens and high-decibel alarms (e.g., from dump trucks and bulldozers) not be used in proximity to his tent;

near him.

At the outset, I acknowledge that the March 2, 2026 Notice of Encampment Closure establishes that
enforcement activity is imminent in the defined geographic area. The City has therefore evaluated these
requests in the context of a pending action. I have assessed each requested accommodation and identified
alternatives where feasible.
1. Safe, Quiet Relocation Option (Non-Congregate Shelter or Designated Safe Location)
Shelter placement is not itself a reasonable accommodation under Title II of the ADA. The provision of
shelter is a public service that may be available depending on eligibility, capacity, and prioritization criteria.
The ADA requires reasonable modifications to policies to ensure equal access to an existing program; it does
not require the creation of new housing resources or the guarantee of a specific housing outcome.
Once Mr. Coon is offered non-congregate shelter through the City’s Homeless Response Team (HRT), he may
request reasonable modifications related to the shelter environment itself, and those would be evaluated in
that context.
No sanctioned campsite exists in Berkeley, and there are no enforcement-free zones. The City’s
encampment-related requirements are established through municipal code provisions, administrative
regulations, and City Council resolutions that apply citywide and are subject to change based on public
health, safety, and legal considerations. Enforcement determinations are location-specific and conditiondependent.
As an alternative, Mr. Coon is encouraged to contact HRT to learn about currently available shelter
opportunities. He may also contact 211 or visit this link: https://berkeleyca.gov/safety-health/homelessservices for information regarding shelter and homeless resources.
2. Modification of Enforcement Operations (Noise Triggers)
You request that sirens, high-decibel alarms, and other loud equipment not be used in proximity to
Mr. Coon during abatement operations. Compliance with the posted Notice, by vacating the defined area by
March 9, 2026, would eliminate proximity to any equipment used during the abatement.
In addition, the City cannot agree to disable, suppress, or refrain from using required safety equipment
during abatement operations. Heavy machinery such as dump trucks and loaders are legally required to
utilize audible backup alarms and other warning systems to protect workers and members of the public.
These features are mandated by safety standards and municipal risk management practices.
Modifying enforcement operations by silencing required alarms or altering standard safety protocols would
constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s health and safety operations. The purpose of these audible
warnings is to prevent serious injury or death. Removing or modifying them for a particular individual would
materially change how the City conducts abatement operations and would introduce foreseeable safety
risks to workers, contractors, bystanders, and the individual himself. The ADA does not require a public
entity to implement modifications that fundamentally alter the nature of a program or compromise
essential safety requirements.
The ADA also does not require the City to purchase or provide personal devices as an accommodation in
these circumstances. Nonetheless, as a discretionary, one-time measure the City is willing to make available
an existing noise reducing, earmuff style headset for Mr. Coon’s voluntary use during this abatement. This
item is being offered solely because it is already available to the City and presents a reasonable alternative
that does not alter operational safety protocols.
Please confirm whether Mr. Coon would like to use the headset for this operation and, if so, how he would
prefer to retrieve it (e.g., scheduled pickup or delivery at a designated time prior to operations).

4. Temporary Disability Refuge Zone (10×10 Safe Zone at Edge of Abatement Area)
No sanctioned campsite exists in Berkeley at this time, including a designated 10×10 “disability refuge zone”.
The City’s encampment regulations generally prohibit structures exceeding nine square feet on sidewalks
and prohibit lodging or storage of property in certain other locations, including medians and City building
curtilage. In limited circumstances, individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding
nine square feet, provided pedestrian access is maintained and the area is not designated as a no-lodging
zone. Even then, all individuals remain subject to enforcement based on health and safety conditions.
Designating a specific exemption area for Mr. Coon within the abatement zone, or allowing him to remain in
his current location for an indefinite amount of time until housing is secured, would constitute a
fundamental alteration of the City’s enforcement framework. The City must address conditions at this
encampment site, including those associated with ongoing leptospirosis related public health concerns.
Allowing continued occupation, whether indefinitely or beyond the posted deadline, would interfere with
the City’s ability to mitigate those risks and would present a direct threat to public health and safety.
Following posted signage and applicable codes and regulations when relocating will reduce the likelihood of
enforcement in a subsequent location, although enforcement decisions remain condition-dependent.
5. Bridge Housing on City-Owned Lot
No sanctioned campsite exists at this time, including a temporary site specific to Mr. Coon. As you’ve
described “bridge housing on a City-owned lot” would require the creation of a new program involving land
designation, sanitation infrastructure, provisions for security, management, and regulatory adjustments.
The ADA does not require the creation of new programs or housing sites as an accommodation.
As an alternative, Mr. Coon is encouraged to engage with HRT regarding currently available shelter or
housing navigation services, or to contact 211 for regional resource information.
6. Phased Relocation Plan with Peer Support
The City does not offer peer-support services and does not operate a program to develop individualized,
step-by-step relocation schedules for members of the public. The ADA requires reasonable modifications to
existing policies, practices, or procedures where necessary to avoid discrimination on the basis of disability;
it does not require a public entity to create entirely new programs or provide services of a personal nature.
Further, providing individualized peer-support navigation or step-by-step relocation planning would
constitute the provision of personal services and the creation of a new programmatic offering, rather than a
modification of an existing policy. Such a requirement would therefore amount to a fundamental alteration
of the City’s programs and exceed the scope of the ADA’s reasonable modification mandate. The type of
peer support described is commonly provided by disability advocacy organizations. Mr. Coon may wish to
contact the Center for Independent Living (CIL) for that assistance at (510) 841-4776 or info@thecil.org.

pedestrian clearance is maintained, cannot be granted. Public sidewalks serve multiple legally protected
purposes, including pedestrian travel (and associated accessibility compliance obligations), emergency
