I would appreciate if the following message could be forwarded to Judge Beeler. 
Dear Judge Beeler: 
Tomorrow, January 29, 2026 is the day by which you have ordered Plaintiff Berkeley Homeless Union to provide 
responses, wihout objection, to Interrogatories and Requests for Production of Documents that were served on 
September 12, 2025. The responses were due on October 14, 2025. 
I will not be able to comply with your order. 
As I tried to explain at the hearing preceding your order, on October 6, 2025 I had an accident and sustained three 
fractures in my left shoulder. On the same day I promptly notified City Attorney Marc Shapp about the injury and told him 
that I would be unable to provide the Union's portion of a status report that was coming due. 
Shortly thereafter, I tried to explain in the hearing, aware that responses to the City's discovery were due in a matter of 
days I verbally told Mr. Shapp that the Union objected to the City's requests to identify the members of the Berkeley 
Homeless Union and disclose other internal Union information, citing to the Supreme Court's decision in NAACP v. 
Alabama. It is true that I did not put these objections in writing, but Mr. Shapp cannot deny that I made the Union's 
position clear to him both before and after the deadline to respond came and went. 
Shortly thereafter, I informed Mr. Shapp that the Union would make an effort to respond to the discovery and requested 
that the City not consider objections waived. 
At all times, the City was aware that it was physically impossible for me to use my left arm and hand. After providing 
medical documentation to Judge Chen, the deadline to file the joint status report and the preliminary injunction ordered on 
June 10, 2025 was extended for 8 weeks. 
I do not believe at the hearing preceding your order that these facts were made clear. 
The City of Berkeley early on tried and failed to show that the Berkeley Homeless Union lacked standing to bring suit. 
Although it did not appeal Judge Chen's finding that the Union had associational standing to sue on behalf of its members, 
the City has nevertheless continued its efforts to discredit the Union and compel production of information that is protected 
under the First Amendment freedom of speech and association. 
The City already knows the identities of those who are protected under the June 10 limited preliminary injunction because 
membership in the Union, residency in the encampment on the day the injunction was issued and having an application 
for disability accommodations on file were requirements in order to be covered by the injunction. The City has no 
legitimate reason to seek the identities of other Union members and discover protected information about the Union.

## ER-909

Over the ten years that the Callifornia Homeless Union has been organizing, we have been extremely mindful of the risks 
faced by our members at a time when homeless persons have often been criminalized and even defined as terrorists by 
the federal government. BHU is one of dozens of locals and thousands of members affiliated with the California Homeless 
Union Statewide Organizing Council. 
In many cases, Union officers and members have been targeted by cities and counties as we resist the repressive, violent 
sweeps and other measures that have proliferated across this state. This includes the City of Berkeley which in filings in 
this lawsuit has falsely accused our members of making death threats against City officials. 
I was hoping at the hearing preceding your order to argue that we did timely object to interrogatories and requests for 
documents that would disclose the internal workings and members of BHU. Instead, the hearing was focused on whether 
or not I had the current ability to answer discovery. 
I have an obligation to protect the Union and its members. I cannot provide information to the City of Berkeley which has 
shown undisguised hostility and police violence against our members, including the surprise attempt to close the 8th and 
Harrison encampment by Berkeley Police on June 4, 2025, which Judge Chen ordered halted and. in an explanatory 
order issued the next day, condemned as a serious violation of due process that if not halted, would have rendered moot 
a hearing on BHU's motion for injunctive relief scheduled on shortened time a few days later. 
I write to request leave to file a motion for reconsideration of the court's ruling that BHU answer the City's discovery 
without objection and an opportunity to fully explain and argue that objections were timely made and should not be 
waived. I write to request leave to file a motion for a protective order. 
I want to note that I have never disobeyed a court order in 24 years of practicing law and have never been threatrened 
with or held in contempt. But I cannot jeopardize the rights and safety of BHU members by providing information to the 
City which may be used to do harm to my clients. 
Should there be any question about the legitimacy of the BHU as an organization, I would be happy to provide to the 
Court for its review in camera the information and documents that the City requests. 
Respectfully, 
Anthony Prince, 
Attorney for Berkeley Homeless Union
