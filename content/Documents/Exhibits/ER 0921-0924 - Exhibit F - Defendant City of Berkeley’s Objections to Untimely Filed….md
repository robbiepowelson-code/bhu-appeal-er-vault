Defendant City of Berkeley (“City”) objects to the “Exhibits” filed in support of Plaintiff 
Berkeley Homeless Union’s (“BHU’s”) combined motion for summary judgment and opposition 
to the City’s motion for summary judgment ([[ER 1063-1063 - Caption Page for Exhibits A-Z to Plaintiff’s Berkeley Homeless Union’s…|ECF No. 253]]) and other purported evidence 
submitted in support of the motion. 
Plaintiff filed eighteen Exhibits, unaccompanied by a declaration or other basis for 
authenticating the exhibits, on February 24, 2026. The Exhibits were filed four days after the 
already untimely submission and Plaintiff’s combined motion and opposition brief. The deadline 
to file Plaintiff’s brief had already been extended twice without Defendants’ objection. However, 
Plaintiff failed to seek leave for a third extension and failed to submit any supporting evidence at 
the time its brief was filed. 
Defendant objects to Plaintiff’s Exhibits in their entirety on the grounds stated below, 
and further objects to the purported declaration of BHU representative Yesica Prado, whose 
declaration is apparently cited in BHU’s brief but was not filed. 
1. The City objects to the Exhibits on the ground that Plaintiff failed to comply with 
the Local Rules or the Court’s Scheduling Order. 
Plaintiff’s exhibits were submitted late, five days after the filing deadline, without a 
request for leave. The Court has discretion to strike any late-filed papers and to refuse to 
consider “late-submitted evidence at summary judgment.” Re/Max Mega Grp. v. Maxum Indem. 
Co., No. CV 09–06310 DDP (CTX), 2010 WL 11507373, at *4 (C.D. Cal. Sept. 24, 2010), aff'd, 
471 F. App'x 689 (9th Cir. 2012) (quoting Cusano v. Klein, 264 F.3d 936, 951 (9th Cir. 2001).) 
Here, Plaintiff sought leave twice to extend the deadline to file summary judgment papers, 
without objection by the City. Plaintiff did not meet the twice-extended February 19 deadline to 
file his summary judgment papers. Plaintiff did not seek leave to file late papers and has offered 
no reason for failing to meet the deadline. 
Plaintiff’s late submission is also improper under Civil Local Rule 7-3, which requires all 
evidence supporting a motion to be filed with the motion itself (which was also filed late, albeit 
only by one day). The rule does not permit supplemental evidence unless authorized by the 
Court or submitted in reply to address points raised in opposition. Plaintiff’s filing does neither.

Plaintiff submitted evidence well after the filing deadline, depriving Defendant of a fair 
opportunity to address the new evidence within the established briefing schedule. 
The Court should strike Plaintiff’s late-filed Exhibits to avoid prejudice to the City. The 
City prepared its response to Plaintiff’s brief within the accelerated schedule set by the Court 
and filed its brief on time. While ordinarily prejudice to the City could be cured by an extension 
of filing deadlines, the City would suffer even greater prejudice if the deadlines in this case were 
extended, given that an injunction is in place that precludes the City from fully addressing public 
nuisance conditions and public health hazards in the Harrison Street encampment. The Court’s 
reasoning in establishing the expedited summary judgment briefing schedule warrants enforcing 
that schedule. See Johnson v. Mammoth Recreations, Inc., 975 F.2d 604, 610 (9th Cir. 1992) 
(citation and internal quotation marks omitted) (“A scheduling order is not a frivolous piece of 
paper, idly entered, which can be cavalierly disregarded by counsel without peril.”). 
The Court has already been lenient in allowing Plaintiff two extensions to submit its 
summary judgement papers. The Court should not ignore Plaintiff’s unexcused failure to meet 
the extended deadline. The Court should strike Plaintiff’s Exhibits in their entirety and decline to 
consider the Exhibits in support of Plaintiff’s motion and opposition. 
2. The City objects to the Exhibits on the ground that they lack authentication. 
Plaintiff has not submitted declarations or any other evidence that would establish the 
authenticity of the eighteen exhibits filed on February 24. The City objects to the Exhibits in 
their entirety on that basis. 
3. The City objects to the Exhibits on the ground that they are hearsay. 
The Exhibits are hearsay statements, and Plaintiff has provided no foundation for their 
admissibility under an exception to the hearsay rule. The City objects to the Exhibits in their 
entirety on that basis. 
4. The City objects to the purported declaration of Yesica Prado on the grounds 
that the declaration was not filed and that her testimony lacks foundation and 
is inadmissible hearsay.
