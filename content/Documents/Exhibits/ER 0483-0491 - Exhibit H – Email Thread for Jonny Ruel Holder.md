Notice and Alleged Delay
The encampment at 6th and Harrison has been on notice since January 2025. Multiple subsequent notices
have been posted. In addition, City staff have repeatedly visited the encampment, particularly after the
discovery of leptospirosis-related public health concerns, to encourage individuals to relocate away from
the area for their own health and safety.
Your statement that Mr. Holder approached HRT on February 5, 2026, prior to the most recent abatement
notice, further confirms that this is not a short-notice situation.
With respect to the assertion of “delay in proper guidance,” a reasonable accommodation must relate to
the application of an existing policy, practice, or enforcement action. The City cannot modify or suspend a
policy that has not yet been applied. Prior to the issuance of a posted notice or enforcement directive, there
is no specific policy application to modify. Moreover, Mr. Holder has contacted me directly several times
since at least 2025 regarding other accommodation matters and is familiar with how to submit requests.
Under these circumstances, there was no denial of access to the accommodation process.
You also state that Mr. Holder did not receive a copy of the public notice. The City has attempted to post
and maintain individual notices on at least three occasions. However, staff have observed that individual
notices have repeatedly been removed or destroyed by a Berkeley Homeless Union member. This limits the
City’s ability to ensure continuous physical posting. When I attempted to speak directly with
Mr. Holder earlier this week regarding his reasonable accommodation needs, he did not indicate that he
had not received notice or request a copy. If he would like a copy delivered personally or sent electronically,
please let me know and I will provide one promptly. A copy of the notice is attached here as well.
Requested Accommodations
a) Interim Housing Placement (hotel or private non-congregate unit)
The ADA requires reasonable modifications to policies where necessary to avoid disability-based
discrimination. The City maintains that the provisions of shelter or housing itself is not a form of reasonable
accommodation under the ADA. However, once a City-sponsored shelter placement becomes available, the
City is obligated to consider reasonable accommodations related to the shelter environment itself, to ensure
equal access to that program.
When shelter placement is offered to Mr. Holder, he may request a disability-related modification in that
context. At present, the ADA does not require the City to provide a specific housing outcome, such as a
privately billed hotel room in another jurisdiction, or to bypass established shelter eligibility and
prioritization systems absent a showing that those systems deny equal access because of disability.
Shelter options are available that would accept his service animal. I understand from prior communications
that Mr. Holder has declined both congregate and non-congregate shelter options within the City and has
expressed interest only in permanent supportive housing and a private hotel room in San Francisco in the
interim. I continue to encourage him to engage with HRT to explore available options and to request
modifications in that context if needed.

There are no enforcement-free areas within the City. The City reiterates that it does not maintain
sanctioned encampment areas and that its encampment regulations are reflected in municipal code
provisions, administrative regulations, and City Council resolutions that apply citywide and are subject to
change. The City’s encampment-related requirements are established through a combination of municipal
code provisions, administrative regulations, and City Council resolutions, all of which may be updated in
response to public health conditions, safety concerns, and legal requirements.
For example:
Park rules prohibit overnight camping and unattended property exceeding ten square feet without a
permit.
The Berkeley Municipal Code and Administrative Regulation 10.2 generally prohibit structures
exceeding nine square feet on sidewalks.
Those provisions also prohibit lodging or storage of property on medians and within City building
curtilage.
In limited circumstances, individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding nine
square feet, provided pedestrian access is maintained, facilities are not obstructed, and the location is not
designated as a no-lodging area. Even in those circumstances, individuals remain subject to enforcement
based on health and safety impacts, nuisance conditions, and compliance with applicable law.
Because enforcement determinations are location-specific, condition-dependent, and responsive to
changing circumstances, including sanitation, obstruction, emergency access, and public health factors, it is
not possible for the City to identify locations where camping would be categorically free from enforcement.
Creating a designated enforcement-free zone for one individual would require suspending generally
applicable municipal code provisions and administrative regulations in a defined geographic area. That
would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s regulatory framework and is not required by the
ADA. As an alternative, I have previously provided a summary of applicable rules.
c) 30 Days’ Advance Written Notice of Any Enforcement
The request for a guaranteed 30-day advance written notice before any future enforcement action seeks a
categorical modification of the City’s enforcement timelines.
The City already provides notice periods that exceed minimum constitutional and statutory requirements.
These extended notice periods are intended, in part, to account for the fact that individuals with disabilities
may require additional time to review, process, and comply.
Imposing a mandatory 30-day delay in all circumstances would:
1. Create an Undue Administrative Burden – The City must respond to evolving conditions, including
blocked rights of way, fire hazards, infrastructure damage, and sanitation failures. A fixed 30-day delay
would require the City to suspend standard enforcement timelines and track individualized notice
guarantees across changing conditions, which would not be operationally feasible on a citywide basis.
2. Fundamentally Alter Enforcement Authority – The City’s health and safety enforcement framework
depends on the ability to respond proportionally and promptly to conditions on the ground. A
guaranteed delay, regardless of circumstances, would substantially alter how the City carries out its
legal obligations.

3. Undermine Public Health Response – In light of the leptospirosis-related conditions at this
location, the City must retain the ability to act quickly to mitigate health risks and the spread
of the disease. The ADA does not require modifications that would create or perpetuate a
direct threat to the health or safety of others. A fixed 30-day delay, especially where urgent
conditions exist, would undermine that obligation.
For these reasons, this request cannot be granted.
d) Visual Map of Permissible Locations
Because there are no sanctioned encampments or enforcement-free zones, and because enforcement
decisions depend on specific, evolving conditions rather than fixed geographic boundaries, the City cannot
provide a map identifying areas where tents are categorically permitted.
As an alternative, I have provided summaries of applicable rules.
e) Recognition of Tent as Medical Equipment
The City does not intend to seize or destroy Mr. Holder’s tent. The purpose of advance notice is to allow
voluntary relocation before any abatement activity occurs.
The ADA does not require the City to designate a tent located on public property as medical equipment
exempt from generally applicable health and safety laws. However, consistent with established practice, the
City provides notice specifically to allow individuals to move with their belongings intact.
Mr. Holder has been aware of the abatement process since at least February 5 and has been repeatedly
encouraged to engage with HRT regarding relocation and shelter options.
f) Physical Assistance with Relocation
You state that Mr. Holder requires assistance to avoid physical strain. However, no physical mobility
impairment or lifting limitation has been identified, and Mr. Holder has not previously indicated such
limitations in our discussions.
A reasonable accommodation requires a clear nexus between the disability and the requested modification.
As such, that nexus has not been established.
g) Temporary Stay-in-Place
The City cannot grant a temporary pause in enforcement at this location. The encampment conditions
contributing to the leptospirosis-related public health concerns must be addressed.
Allowing continued occupation at this site would delay mitigation of sanitation and hinder the ability of the
city to address the extensive rodent population that is the primary vector for leptospirosis. Where a
requested modification would prevent the City from addressing conditions that pose a direct threat to
health and safety, the ADA does not require that modification.
Given the length of prior notice and outreach, there is no ADA-based basis for extending occupancy at this
location in a manner that would interfere with public health response.
h) ADA-Exempted 12’×12’ Footprint on Public Sidewalks
The request for a permanent 140-square-foot footprint on any public sidewalk, provided a four-foot
pedestrian path is maintained, cannot be granted.
Public sidewalks serve multiple legally protected functions:
