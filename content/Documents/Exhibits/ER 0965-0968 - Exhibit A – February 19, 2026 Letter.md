Counsel:
Plaintiffs in Prado v. City of Berkeley received a February 17, 2026 notice from the City 
of Berkeley requiring them to vacate the 8th and Harrison Encampment and the
surrounding one-third mile radius by or before February 24, 2026, because the area has 
been deemed a no-lodging area and Temporary Non-Commercial Item Prohibited Area. 
The City does not identify a location where encampment residents can safely relocate
and states that it will not store any property left behind due to the leptospirosis outbreak.
The Notice states that the Berkeley Public Health Officer has “advised it is hazardous to 
sort through or store items from this encampment,” meaning their possessions are too 
hazardous for the City to even sort through before destruction. The City has also failed 
to articulate any plan to replace residents’ tents or survival gear. The Notice also 
removes language included in prior notices informing encampment residents of their 
right to seek disability accommodations, despite the fact that encampment residents 
with disabilities are at the greatest risk of harm as a result of the City’s actions.
If the City proceeds with its intended course of action, Plaintiffs and the Putative Class 
will be placed in state-created danger in violation of the Fourteenth Amendment and will 
be deprived of their property in violation of the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments and 
the California Constitution.
The City of Berkeley is currently facing widespread rain and near-record cold 
temperatures, which are expected to continue through the middle of next week, 
including the City’s noticed compliance deadline of February 24th. Despite the 
dangerous weather conditions, the City ordered encampment residents to pack up their 
tents and survival gear and vacate the area, without designating a safe relocation site. 
The Notice also states that any vehicles remaining in the area are subject to immediate 
tow and impoundment. RV residents rely on their vehicles as their only shelter from the 
elements and this threat of towing poses an acute risk of irreparable harm. By forcing 
residents to leave behind their shelter during a storm and without any safe alternative

The City’s planned response to the leptospirosis outbreak will also place encampment 
residents in state-created danger. Courts have regularly intervened when cities’ actions 
during infectious disease outbreaks have endangered encampment residents. For 
example, in Santa Cruz Homeless Union v. Bernal, the Court issued a preliminary 
injunction preventing the closure of a camp during the COVID-19 Pandemic, 
determining that closure of the camp would place residents in state-created danger.
514 F. Supp. 3d 1136, 1146 (N.D. Cal. 2021). In granting the preliminary injunction, the 
Court referenced the lack of shelter or placement options as well as the fact that in 
previous encampment closures, residents had lost survival gear and shelter, leaving 
them exposed to the elements and with greater exposure to disease. Id. at 1144-45.
Here, closing the encampment without providing a place to go and any protective 
equipment or replacement survival gear places residents in similar danger. According to 
the City’s own filings in Berkeley Homeless Union v. City of Berkeley, No. 3:25-cv01414-EMC (N.D. Cal.), leptospirosis spreads through animal urine, particularly rats,
and via accumulations of contaminated water. ECF No. 194-3 at ¶¶ 4, 9. The current 
and incoming rain will only create more accumulations of water in which the bacteria 
can spread. Rather than providing encampment residents with assistance moving their 
belongings, personal protective equipment, hygiene supplies, replacement survival 
gear, or a safe place to go, the City proposes to leave each encampment resident to 
their own devices. This will force residents to sort through items that are supposedly too 
likely to be infected with leptospirosis for City personnel to even touch (with none of the 
resources available to the City), and to then take their sodden and supposedly-infected
possessions to some unspecified other place, likely in another part of the City of 
Berkeley. Not only does this risk the health of encampment residents, but, if the City’s 
concerns are taken at face value, it also increases the risk of spreading leptospirosis to 
other areas of the City. Given the City’s representations about the “imminent risk 
present to human health” of leptospirosis, see id. ¶ 16, the City must protect 
encampment residents by assisting with relocation of essential items, replacing 
essential survival gear that cannot be safely saved, providing personal protective 
equipment and hygiene supplies as necessary, and designating a safe relocation site
where the community can move.
In the February 17, 2026 Notice, the City also indicated that all property remaining at 
the encampment will be discarded due to the potential for leptospirosis contamination. 
The City’s planned action violates the Fourth Amendment and the California 
Constitution, which prohibit the seizure and destruction of unabandoned personal 
property. Lavan v. City of Los Angeles, 693 F.3d 1022, 1030 (9th Cir. 2012). Absent a 
determination that particular items have been contaminated with rodent urine possibly

Plaintiffs and the Putative Class demand that the City:
1. Postpone the planned February 24, 2026, encampment closure until 72 hours 
pass without rain to allow residents’ belongings to dry;
2. Designate a safe relocation site or sites for current residents of the 8th and 
Harrison Encampment with assurances that residents will not be swept from such 
site(s), either permanently or until it is safe for residents to return to 8th and 
Harrison;
3. Provide assistance to individuals living in vehicles to relocate with their vehicles 
to the designated safe relocation site; and
4. Replace all essential survival gear that the City intends to dispose of, including 
but not limited to tents, tarps, bedding, clothing, cooking supplies, food, and 
mobility aids.
If the City does not accede to these demands, Plaintiffs will file a Motion for Temporary 
Restraining Order.
