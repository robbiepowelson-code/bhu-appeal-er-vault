Hi Yesica,
Thank you for reaching out on behalf of Mr. Quinton Cocklereese.
You have submitted substantially similar requests on behalf of numerous individuals over the past several
months, and you have previously been advised that such requests would constitute a fundamental
alteration of the City’s operations, impose an undue burden, or, in the case of shelter, do not constitute a
form of reasonable accommodation under the ADA, regardless of any particular individual’s disabilities.
Under Title II of the ADA, the City may deny requests based on fundamental alteration or undue burden,
because of the impact that a particular request would have on the City and its programs or services. As I
have previously informed you, those impacts to the City are the same, regardless of the individual’s
disability or the nexus for their request. As such, even if there were a nexus between Mr. Cocklereese’s
requests and his required relocation, the following determinations would still apply.
The determinations below, including alternatives where feasible, were made by me in consultation with the
City Manager’s Office and relevant City departments and staff.
Re your first enumerated request (“Executive Functioning Assistance”), the City does not provide such a
service, and Title II of the ADA does not require that the City create a new service. Center for Independent
Living, in Berkeley, provides no-cost peer support to folks with executive functioning deficits.
https://thecil.org/
Re your second enumerated request (“Designated Point of Contact”), Mr. Cocklereese may contact me at
(510) 981-6418 and/or at ada@cityofberkeley.info if he has questions.
Re your third enumerated request (“Advance Written Notice with Step-by-Step Instructions”), the City is
unable to grant a request for 30 days’ notice to vacate the encampment. Providing that extended notice
would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s encampment management program because it
would prevent the City from effectively addressing public health hazards, including the presence of
Leptospira identified at the encampment.
The requested 30-day notice would also fundamentally alter the City’s ability to implement its encampment
management program in a way that flexibly responds to health and safety conditions in the community. The
presence of Leptospira at the encampment illustrates the types of public health risks that require the City to

Re your fourth enumerated request (“Low-Stimulation Meetings”), the City is not requiring Mr.
Cocklereese to attend any meetings. Whenever Mr. Cocklereese arranges a meeting with a City staff person,
he is welcome to contact me to explain the specific requests he has to minimize the possibility that he will

meeting, consistent with my duties as the City’s ADA Coordinator.
Re your fifth enumerated request (“Safe Relocation Option”), no sanctioned campsite exists in Berkeley at
this time. The City’s encampment regulations generally prohibit structures exceeding nine square feet on
sidewalks and prohibit lodging or storage of property in certain other locations, including medians and City
building curtilage. Individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding nine square
feet, provided pedestrian access is maintained and the area is not designated as a no-lodging zone. Even
then, all individuals remain subject to enforcement based on health and safety conditions.
Following posted signage and applicable codes and regulations when relocating will reduce the likelihood of
enforcement in a subsequent location, although enforcement decisions remain condition dependent. If Mr.
Cocklereese is seeking assistance or services, he may contact HRT or visit https://berkeleyca.gov/safetyhealth/homeless-services for information about homeless services that may be available to him.
Re your sixth enumerated request (“Non-Congregate Shelter Placement”), shelter placement is not itself a
reasonable accommodation under Title II of the ADA. The provision of shelter is a public service that may be
available depending on eligibility, capacity, and prioritization criteria. The ADA requires reasonable
modifications to policies to ensure equal access to an existing program; it does not require the creation of
new housing resources or the guarantee of shelter.
Once Mr. Cocklereese is offered shelter through the City’s Homeless Response Team (HRT), he may request
reasonable modifications related to the shelter environment itself, and those would be evaluated in that
context. Mr. Cocklereese is encouraged to contact HRT to learn about currently available non-congregate
shelter opportunities. He may also contact 211 for information regarding regional resources.
Re your seventh enumerated request (“Temporary Stay-in-Place Pending Accessible Housing”), individuals
remain subject to relocation based on health and safety conditions. Designating a specific exemption area
for Mr. Cocklereese within the abatement zone, or allowing him to remain in his current location for an
indefinite amount of time until housing is secured, would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s
encampment management program. The City must address conditions at this encampment site, including
those associated with ongoing leptospirosis related public health concerns. Allowing
indefinite occupation would interfere with the City’s ability to mitigate those risks and would present a
direct threat to public health and safety.
Further, designating a specific exemption area for Mr. Cocklereese for an indefinite amount of time until
housing is secured would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s encampment management
program. The City must address conditions at this encampment site, including those associated with
ongoing leptospirosis related public health concerns. Allowing continued indefinite occupation would
interfere with the City’s ability to mitigate those risks and would present a direct threat to public health and
safety. As an alternative, the City will grant Mr. Cocklereese an accommodation to remain through March
10, 2026. The City cannot grant additional time without causing the fundamental alteration I have explained
above.

Re your eighth enumerated request (“Recognition of Dog as Medical Equipment”), dogs are not
equipment, medical or otherwise. To the extent that the animal(s) in question are service animals then
they may go/be anywhere that Mr. Cocklereese may go/be. To the extent that the animal(s) in question are
emotional support animals then they may accompany Mr. Cocklereese to the extent that he is actively

from a qualified professional confirming that the animal(s) in question are necessary for Mr. Cocklereese’s
mental wellbeing.

Cocklereese’s behalf, and this determination is based upon the reasons described above, and due to the fact

requested accommodation, and because the requested accommodation would impede actions necessary to
protect public health. If Mr. Cocklereese disagrees with this determination, he may choose to file a
