I am writing on behalf of Monique Williams, a disabled member of the Berkeley Homeless Union currently residing in a
van at the east corner of 8th and Harrison Streets, to formally submit a request for reasonable accommodations.
Please find below the information required to initiate this request:
1. Disability Verification
Ms. Williams lives with documented disabilities, including:
Physical Disabilities:
Hip injury that limits physical movement
Metal pins in her arms, restricting mobility and strength
Mental Health Disabilities:
Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
Impaired short-term memory resulting from being hit in the back of the head with a gun
Other mental health issues stemming from trauma
These conditions substantially limit one or more major life activities, including walking, standing, lifting, carrying, sleeping,
concentrating, remembering, regulating emotions and feeling safe.
Ms. Williams receives care from the Lifelong Street Medical Team. We are in the process of obtaining medical verification
from her provider and will forward it to you as soon as it becomes available. Given the urgency of the enforcement
timeline, we ask that the City proceed with the interactive process based on the disabilities self-reported in her signed
accommodation request form (attached), pending receipt of formal verification.

The City's standard encampment management policies—as outlined in the February 17, 2026 Public Notice—will, if
applied to Ms. Williams without modification, foreseeably aggravate her disabilities in the following ways:
a) Short-notice relocation
Her PTSD and memory impairment require predictability and adequate time to process information. Sudden displacement
triggers hypervigilance, panic, and emotional dysregulation, leaving her unable to plan or comply with City orders. Her
memory impairment means she may forget deadlines or instructions without repeated, clear communication.
b) Physical demands of relocation
Due to her hip injury and metal pins, Ms. Williams has limited mobility and cannot lift heavy objects, walk long distances,

Due to her hip injury and metal pins, Ms. Williams requires City staff assistance with lifting and transporting her essential

5) Storage for Essential Belongings

Hi Yesica,
You have submitted substantially similar requests on behalf of numerous individuals over the past
several months, and you have previously been advised that such requests would constitute a
fundamental alteration of the City’s operations, impose an undue burden, or, in the case of shelter,
do not constitute a form of reasonable accommodation under the ADA, regardless of any particular
individual’s disabilities. Under Title II of the ADA, the City may deny requests based on
fundamental alteration or undue burden, because of the impact that a particular request would
have on the City and its programs or services. As I have previously informed you, those impacts to
the City are the same, regardless of the individual’s disability or the nexus for their request. The
determination below was made by me in consultation with the City Manager’s Office and relevant
City departments and staff.
The City has reviewed your recent request for reasonable accommodations on behalf of Ms.
Williams. The City is highly concerned that the email misrepresents significant facts regarding Ms.
Williams’ circumstances and the accommodations being requested. According to City records and
recent staff observations, Ms. Williams is currently enrolled as a participant at the Campus Motel
and resides there. Because your request appears to misrepresent these facts, and the City cannot
rely on the representations made in your correspondence, the City will not grant the requested
accommodations.
As I have repeatedly informed you, to the extent Ms. Williams seeks an accommodation to park her
vehicle in a manner that would violate California Vehicle Code registration requirements, the City is
unable to grant that request. Exempting Ms. Williams’ vehicle from enforcement would result in a
fundamental alteration of the City’s parking enforcement program. The City’s enforcement of local
and State parking provisions is designed to promote public safety, ensure access to limited onstreet parking, facilitate street maintenance, and mitigate neighborhood impacts associated with

Ms. Williams’ vehicle may be towed if it is out of compliance with California registration

The City strives to be flexible with unhoused community members where possible, which is evident

negative community impacts.
