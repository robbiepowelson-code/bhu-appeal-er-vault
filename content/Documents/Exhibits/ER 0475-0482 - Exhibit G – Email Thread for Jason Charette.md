For Mr. Charette, his RV is not merely personal property—it is essential medical equipment that provides:
A private, locked space where he can manage PTSD symptoms without fear of intrusion;
A controlled environment that minimizes stress triggers;
Access to basic sanitation (bathroom) and nutrition (kitchen) , which are critical for managing depression and
maintaining daily function;
The stability and predictability his disabilities require;
Removing him from his RV without a suitable, accessible alternative would constitute denial of medical necessity and
would foreseeably aggravate his mental health disabilities.

Hi Yesica,
Thank you for reaching out on behalf of Mr. Jason Charette. You have submitted substantially similar
requests on behalf of numerous individuals over the past several months, and you have previously been
advised that such requests would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s operations, impose an
undue burden, are based on financial considerations rather than disability-related needs, or, in the case
of shelter, do not constitute a form of reasonable accommodation under the ADA, regardless of any
particular individual’s disabilities. Under Title II of the ADA, the City may deny requests based on
fundamental alteration or undue burden because of the impact that a particular request would have on
the City and its programs or services. As I have previously informed you, those impacts to the City are
the same regardless of the individual’s disability or the nexus for their request. These determinations
were made by me in consultation with the City Manager’s Office and relevant City departments and
staff.
Regarding your first enumerated request (“A Safe Location to Relocate”), the City’s “no lodging”
prohibitions do not apply to vehicles or RVs that are lawfully parked. Vehicles remain subject to standard
parking enforcement rules applicable citywide. Any required movement of vehicles or RVs is directed
through applicable parking regulations or temporary signage. Mr. Charette is free to park on Berkeley
streets, the same as any other visitor or resident, in compliance with the California Vehicle Code and
posted signage.
The City’s current parking enforcement practices rely primarily on automated license plate
recognition (“ALPR”) technology to identify abandoned vehicles and parking violations.
ALPR data is not retained beyond a limited period, which makes it infeasible to track
compliance with extended or individualized parking timelines. As a result, accommodating
an individualized parking exemption would require a fundamental alteration of standard
enforcement practices.
Specifically, parking enforcement staff would be required to rely on manual and laborintensive methods, such as maintaining paper logs of protected vehicles, physically
marking vehicles, returning to inspect those markings, and resolving disputes regarding
whether a vehicle has moved. These manual methods lack the permanence, reliability,
and data integrity of ALPR-based enforcement and in other contexts have led to
increased disputes with members of the public regarding compliance.
Further, requiring parking and law enforcement officers to remain continually aware of
specific vehicles subject to individualized rules would significantly complicate routine
enforcement operations and divert limited resources from the City’s broader parking,
traffic, and safety responsibilities. For these reasons, the City concludes that granting the
requested exemption would impose an undue administrative burden.

Regarding your second enumerated request (“Non-Congregate Shelter
Placement”), shelter placement is not itself a reasonable accommodation under Title II of
the ADA. The provision of shelter is a public service that may be available depending on
eligibility, capacity, and prioritization criteria. The ADA requires reasonable modifications
to policies to ensure equal access to an existing program; it does not require the creation
of new housing resources or the guarantee of a specific housing outcome.
Once Mr. Charette is offered shelter through the City’s Homeless Response Team (HRT),
he may request reasonable modifications related to the shelter environment itself, and
those would be evaluated in that context. If Mr. Charette is seeking assistance or
services, he may contact HRT or visit https://berkeleyca.gov/safety-health/homelessservices for information about homeless services that may be available to him.
Mr. Charette is encouraged to contact HRT to learn about currently available noncongregate shelter opportunities. He may also contact 211 for information regarding
regional resources.
Regarding your third enumerated request (“Temporary Stay-in-Place Pending
Accessible Housing”), all individuals remain subject to enforcement based on health
and safety conditions. Designating a specific exemption area for Mr. Charette within the
abatement zone, or allowing him to remain in his current location for an indefinite amount
of time until housing is secured, would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s
enforcement framework. The City must address conditions at this encampment site,
including those associated with ongoing leptospirosis related public health concerns.
Allowing continued occupation, whether indefinitely or beyond the posted deadline, would
interfere with the City’s ability to mitigate those risks and would present a direct threat to
public health and safety. As an alternative, the City will grant Mr. Charette an
accommodation to remain through March 10, 2026.
Regarding your fourth enumerated request (“Recognition of RV as Medical
Equipment”), as I have informed you in the past, reasonable accommodations under the
ADA involve modifications to a public entity’s policies, practices, or procedures. The City
does not maintain a policy that classifies vehicles or recreational vehicles as any
particular type of equipment, and therefore there is no City policy applicable to this
request that could be modified through the reasonable accommodation process.
Classifications such as “recreational vehicle” are established by state and federal law,
including vehicle registration and regulatory frameworks under the California Vehicle
Code, not by municipal designation. Because the City does not determine how vehicles
are classified under those legal frameworks, it cannot issue a formal acknowledgment that
Mr. Charette’s RV constitutes medical equipment. Additionally, the request does not
identify a nexus between Mr. Charette’s disabilities and any modification to a City policy,
program, or procedure.
Regarding your fifth enumerated request (“Advance Written Notice of Any
Enforcement”), the City is unable to grant a request for 30 days’ notice to vacate the
encampment. Providing that extended notice would constitute a fundamental alteration of
the City’s encampment management program because it would prevent the City from
effectively addressing public health hazards, including the presence of Leptospira
identified at the encampment. As part of its ongoing encampment management program,
the City has repeatedly provided both written and verbal notices regarding the need to
relocate since January 2025. Your email below cites that Mr. Charette has been on notice
since at least February 17, 2026, over two weeks ago, which the City has determined is
sufficient, to the extent he requires additional time to comply.
Regarding your sixth enumerated request (“Visual Aid / Map of Permissible RV
Locations”), visual signage throughout the City already makes clear where vehicles

Regarding your seventh enumerated request (“Exemption from RV Towing and
Impoundment”), exempting Mr. Charette’s vehicle from the enforcement of parking rules
would result in a fundamental alteration of the City’s parking enforcement program. The
City’s enforcement of local and State provisions governing parking is designed to promote
public safety, ensure access to limited on-street parking, facilitate street maintenance, and
mitigate neighborhood impacts associated with long-term vehicle storage. Exempting Mr.
Charette’s vehicle from towing would undermine and fundamentally alter the purpose and
application of these enforcement provisions and interfere with the City’s ability to manage
community impacts associated with unlawfully parked vehicles. Mr. Charette’s vehicle
may be towed if it is out of compliance with California registration requirements, including
if registration has been expired for more than six months, as authorized under California
Vehicle Code section 22651(o). The City is unable to grant the request for an exemption.
The basis for the request is financial, rather than related to a disability, and therefore does
not qualify as a reasonable accommodation under the ADA. The California Vehicle Code
permits law enforcement to tow vehicles with registration expired for more than six
months, and the City enforces these requirements consistently to ensure public safety
and compliance with state law. The City strives to be flexible with unhoused community
members regardless of its authority to tow, which is evident in that Mr. Charette and
several other members of the encampment have been allowed to park unregistered
vehicles around the City for years without being towed, provided they did not cause
negative community impacts.
Regarding your eighth enumerated request (“ADA-Exempted Footprint”), the
request for an indefinite or permanent footprint on any public sidewalk, even provided a
four-foot pedestrian clearance is maintained, cannot be granted. Public sidewalks serve
multiple legally protected purposes, including pedestrian travel (and associated
accessibility compliance obligations), emergency access, commercial activity conducted
under active City permits, and placement of utilities and public infrastructure. Sidewalk
widths vary considerably, and in many locations a four-foot clearance would not satisfy
accessibility requirements once curb ramps, turning radii, and other obstructions are
considered. Granting Mr. Charette an exemption from the nine-square-foot limitation
would fundamentally alter the City’s management of the public right of way and conflict
with other legally protected uses. For those reasons, this request cannot be approved.
