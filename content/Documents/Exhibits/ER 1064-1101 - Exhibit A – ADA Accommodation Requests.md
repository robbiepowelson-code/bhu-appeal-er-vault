Dear Mr. Gregory,
I am writing on behalf of Ray Johnson Jr. and Vanessa Bichard, members of the Berkeley Homeless Union, to
formally request reasonable accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) due to their
respective disabilities.
Mr. Johnson and Ms. Bichard are both disabled individuals who rely on their vehicle as their primary safety net and
form of shelter. Forced displacement or the removal of their vehicle would significantly destabilize their lives, exacerbate
their disabilities, and create an unreasonable hardship that they cannot mitigate on their own.
Background on Disabilities & Requested Accommodations
Ray Johnson Jr. has PTSD, Bipolar Schizophrenia, depression, and anxiety. Frequent displacement and the threat
of losing his only shelter can trigger psychotic episodes, paranoia, and severe emotional distress. He requires a
stable environment to avoid unnecessary harm to his mental health.
Vanessa Bichard has Asperger’s Syndrome (Autism Spectrum Disorder) and has lived with this condition since
childhood. She struggles with stress and sudden changes, and the city’s abatement actions significantly worsen
her mental health condition. Her vehicle is her only safety net as she works toward securing permanent supportive
housing.
Because their vehicle is their only stable space, they request:
1) Permission to keep their vehicle as shelter in its current location until an ADA-compliant alternative is
available – Given their disabilities, frequent displacement causes extreme stress and worsens their ability to
function.
2) Exemption from relocation requirements – Mr. Johnson cannot frequently relocate without risking mental
health deterioration, and Ms. Bichard requires stability to manage her autism-related sensory needs and
executive function challenges.
3) Protection from vehicle towing or removal – Their car currently does not have plates, as Ms. Bichard is still
making payments on the vehicle and cannot obtain them until the debt is settled. Removing or impounding the
car would result in immediate risk to their lives and create an undue hardship for both individuals.
4) Recognition that Ms. Bichard requires the vehicle as a safety net – She currently stays at the Berkeley Inn, but
frequently visits Harrison Street to spend time with Mr. Johnson, as the shelter does not allow visitors. If their vehicle is
removed, she will lose her only backup housing option.
5) Exemption from citation and arrest related to the abatement order – Because their disability prevents them from
complying with relocation orders and the car was not documented in the city’s report as imposing a health and
safety concern, enforcing penalties against her would constitute disability-based discrimination under Title II of the
ADA. 6) If relocation is required, placement in an ADA-compliant shelter for Mr. Johnson is needed – If the city
provides an alternative, it must include:
A quiet, private space to prevent sensory overload and mental health deterioration.
Storage for personal belongings and medications, as losing them would lead to serious health consequences.
A place to park the car to prevent towing and losing their safety net.

Legal Basis for Accommodation Request
Under Title II of the ADA (28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7)), public entities must reasonably modify policies, practices, or
procedures to accommodate individuals with disabilities unless the city can demonstrate that the modification would
fundamentally alter the nature of the program or impose an undue burden.

Forcing Mr. Johnson to relocate without accommodations could trigger psychotic episodes, worsening his 
psychiatric conditions.
Towing their vehicle would leave them without shelter, creating an immediate crisis and further destabilizing 
their mental health.

discriminatory.
The city has not demonstrated that their vehicle poses a health or safety risk, making its removal 
unnecessary and punitive.

request.
