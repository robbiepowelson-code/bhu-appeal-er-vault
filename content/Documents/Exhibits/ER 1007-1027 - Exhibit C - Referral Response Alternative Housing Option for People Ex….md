INTRODUCTION
This report responds in part to the City Council’s May 20, 2025 referral, “Alternative 
Housing Options for People Experiencing Homelessness,” referring to the City Manager 
“a request for staff to identify a list of potential locations of City-owned and private 
properties (to be leased or acquired) – for the purpose of establishing 24/7 staffed 
shelter sites, including but not limited to indoor/outdoor camping areas, safe RV and car 
park zones, and/or congregate or non-congregate shelters for people experiencing 
homelessness based on the model used at Grayson Street and other models deemed 
successful by the City Manager.”
CURRENT SITUATION AND ITS EFFECTS
City Council’s May 20 referral to the City Manager requested a list of sites, both owned 
by the City and available for purchase or lease on the private market, that could be used 
to identify additional shelter sites for people experiencing unsheltered homelessness in 
Berkeley. The Council’s direction in that referral was to identify sites that could be 
feasible for replicating the Horizon Transitional Village Program shelter and SPARK 
safe parking model (previously co-located on Grayson St.), as well as sites that could 
host other models deemed successful by staff.
With this in mind, staff have compiled the following information in this report:
 Attachment 1 is a compilation of private market listings of all commercial and 
industrial properties that offer a minimum of 5,000 sq ft.
 Attachment 2 is a compilation of motels/hotels currently available for lease or 
purchase;

POSSIBLE FUTURE ACTION
The City Council could use this list to provide staff with further direction as to acceptable 
locations for new programs and program types that should be prioritized for 
consideration. In addition, Council could direct staff to pursue one or more new 
homeless programs at any of the sites listed above, and/or direct staff to return with 
more or different sites.
At this time, staff is pursuing two sites for noncongregate interim housing, and both 
would be funded by external grants: the Sather Motel, a 32-room motel at 1820 
University Ave. which would be leased by County Measure W funds, , as well as 
another motel to be leased by the City’s recently awarded Prop 47 grant. More 
information on these opportunities is provided immediately below. If these funding 
applications are successful, staff will return to Council as soon as possible for all 
necessary approvals.

However, and as previously reported to Council in staff’s July 29 worksession report, 
“Comprehensive Summary of Berkeley’s Homeless Response,” the City is currently 
facing a significant and structural fiscal challenge, with ongoing general fund deficits 
projected at over $20M annually, and state and federal sources that currently support 
Berkeley’s existing programs are at risk under the current federal administration and 
economic environment. Absent new sources of external funding, including and possibly 
Alameda County Measure W, staff do not recommend opening new programs when 
several existing shelter programs face imminent risk of closure in the coming years.
Staff continue to aggressively pursue all available opportunities for external funding. As 
mentioned above, these include the following two opportunities, neither of which would 
impact the City’s General Fund as neither would require local match funding:
1. In June 2025, City staff applied for an $8M grant through the State of California’s 
Proposition 47 Grant Program. This is a no-match grant. Council voted to accept 
the funds on Sept 30, 2025. Proposition 47 provides funding for public agencies 
to provide mental health services, substance use disorder treatment, and 
diversion programs for individuals in the criminal justice system. The City’s award 
will create three years of the Berkeley CareBridge Program, providing post-arrest 
supportive transitional housing and wraparound services for homeless, justiceinvolved individuals. The housing component would be provided through a 
master lease of a motel facility. The grant also requires establishment of a Local 
Advisory Committee that will meet publicly on a quarterly basis to provide 
program updates, hear from the community, and address needs.
2. In August, 2025, Alameda County released a notice of contract opportunity to 
nonprofit partners in the County’s homeless system vendor pool, announcing an 
initiative to rapidly increase new interim housing units that can be used to support 
encampment resolution. City staff worked with Episcopal Community Services 
(ECS), a San Francisco-based nonprofit with extensive experience in homeless 
services, to sponsor ECS’ application for this funding which, if awarded, would 
fund the master-lease of another motel (the Sather Motel, at 1820 University 
Avenue) to create a noncongregate shelter. Staff and ECS proposed that this 
shelter be used to target the large RV encampments in West Berkeley along 
Dwight and Grayson, using existing Measure P allocations for an expanded RV 
buy-back program. Importantly, the City would not be a direct recipient of these 
grant funds, nor would it hold/be party to any motel leases or services contracts, 
all of which will be handled directly by Alameda County.

BACKGROUND
On May 20, 2025, the City Council passed a referral to the City Manager, “Alternative 
Housing Options for People Experiencing Homelessness.” This referral had multiple 
parts, and the current report is responsive to one part of this referral. Staff actions in 
response to other aspects of this referral include:
 On May 28, the City Manager sent a letter on behalf of the Council to the 
Alameda County Board of Supervisors, affirming the four policy principles 
outlined in the May 20, 2025 referral.
 On July 24, 2025, Council voted to support staff recommendations in the report, 
“Recommendations for County Measure W Funding.” The adoptions of these 
recommendations resulted in a letter sent to the Alameda County Board of 
Supervisors outlining Berkeley’s priorities for County Measure W sales tax 
funding.
 On July 29, 2025, at a special meeting, Council received a report from staff, 
“Comprehensive Summary of Berkeley’s Homeless Response System.” This 
report responded to several aspects of Council’s May 20, 2025 referral, including 
an overview of ongoing efforts to address homelessness; a resource needs and 
gaps analysis of the homeless system; a feasibility and cost analysis to support 
new homeless programs; and a geographic assessment of homeless service 
locations in the city. Due to technical difficulties, that special meeting was 
adjourned prior to the conclusion of staff’s presentation on the report. The 
presentation was concluded during a special meeting on September 16, 2025.
 On October 28, 2025, the Council will consider a recommendation from the City 
Manager to send a letter to Alameda County Supervisor Fortunato Bas regarding 
opportunities to partner on regional solutions to unsheltered homelessness.
