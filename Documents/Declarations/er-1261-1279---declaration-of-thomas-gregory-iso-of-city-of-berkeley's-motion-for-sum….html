Parking enforcement staff would be required to rely on manual and labor-intensive methods. These manual methods lack the permanence, reliability, and data integrity of ALPR-based enforcement and in other contexts have led to increased disputes with members of the public regarding compliance. Further, requiring parking and law enforcement officers to remain continually aware of specific vehicles subject to individualized rules would significantly complicate routine enforcement operations and divert limited resources from the City’s broader parking, traffic, and safety responsibilities. The City’s 72-hour relocation requirement is a core component of a generally applicable, regulatory framework governing use of the public right-of-way designed to balance multiple essential public interests. The program depends on uniform, time-limited occupancy rules to ensure regular turnover of curbside space and to prevent the public right-of- way from being used for long-term vehicle storage. The 72-hour relocation requirement functions as a fundamental limitation that preserves the temporary nature of vehicle occupancy in the public right-of-way and enables the City to manage limited curbside space dynamically across competing demands. Time-based limits are one of the City’s primary tools for ensuring equitable access to parking. The program is not designed to accommodate extended or semi-permanent vehicle placement, even on an individualized basis, because doing so would undermine space turnover and the City’s ability to address cumulative neighborhood, safety, and operational impacts. Granting an accommodation that replaces a time-limited occupancy rule with an open-ended or substantially extended relocation interval would do more than adjust how the rule applies to one individual; it would transform the fundamental nature of the rule itself.
