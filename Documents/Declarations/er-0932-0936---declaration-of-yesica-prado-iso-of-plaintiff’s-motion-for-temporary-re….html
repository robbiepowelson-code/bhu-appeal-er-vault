  1. I am a Plaintiff in the above-entitled action. I am over the age of eighteen, and I have personal knowledge of the facts in this declaration. If called as a witness, I could and would competently testify to the matters stated herein. I make this declaration in support of Plaintiffs’ Motion for Temporary Restraining Order.
  2. I live in my vehicle, which is currently parked on 8th Street between Harrison and Gilman Streets. I have lived in this area since about 2018. In the nearly six years that I have lived here, I have seen many encampment residents come and go, but I work hard to get to know my neighbors and have known some residents for years.
  3. In the last year, I have witnessed a large increase in the population of people at 8th and Harrison. Many new people have reported to me that they are coming here after being swept from areas around City Hall in Downtown Berkeley, Ohlone Park, Allston Way and the Ohlone Greenway. Some unhoused people who work during the day or stay elsewhere closer to services they need in the day also come to this area just to sleep at night because they want to be close to community. At this point, I believe there to be approximately 35 to 40 people who seek shelter at 8th and Harrison, but there may be more who pass through and receive temporary support.
  4. I am a qualified person with a disability. I have been diagnosed with Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD). As a result of these disabilities, I need stability and the safety of community to function in my daily life. A large percentage of my neighbors also have disclosed to me that they have disabilities. I have helped many of them seek disability accommodations to various City actions.
  5. I have seen the Notice of Encampment Closure posted by the City of Berkeley on February 17, 2026, February 18, 2026, and February 19, 2026.
  6. On March 2, 2026, the City re-noticed the encampment closure with a compliance deadline of March 9, 2026. This notice was posted throughout the Harrison encampment, including on fences, the encampment banner, and other locations scattered throughout the encampment. I observed that the notice was posted directly on my RV. However, it was not placed on the surrounding dwellings. Given that the encampment is already saturated with flyers for various events from the city, health events, and volunteer clean-ups, it’s hard to distinguish this critical new notice. True and correct photos of where the notices were posted are included as Exhibit A. 7. There were no Tow-Away signs posted throughout the encampment. I did not observe any signs indicating that vehicles would be subject to towing. 8. While I know that the Notice states that I do not have to move myself because of a Preliminary Injunction in the BHU matter, the proposed closure still deeply impacts me. First, it impacts me because I rely on my community for support. Being close to my neighbors provides me with stability and safety necessary for me to navigate life with my disabilities. Second, if the City is claiming that the leptospirosis outbreak is so detrimental, I also want to be somewhere safe, and if the City would tell my community where we can go and not face being swept again, I would go there. 9. The City causes immense harm when it does encampment abatement and closure actions without telling people where to go. I have been subject to numerous evictions and abatement measures carried out by the City. The City has taken and destroyed my belongings without my consent, and I have seen others in my community lose everything, including the tents and vehicles where they were sleeping in, during evictions. Each time, the trauma of seeing my community in pain and our homes destroyed triggers my PTSD, causing flashbacks, panic and emotional collapse. It puts me in a mental health crisis, guaranteed and foreseeable, that the City’s actions deliberately cause. 10. I do not understand what the City wants us to do – if they give people in the encampment nowhere to go and no replacement belongings, people will be forced to wander the streets with what few belongings they can carry – which the City claims may be so hazardous and infected with Leptospirosis as to justify not sorting or storing anything. The City cannot have it both ways: it cannot tell the Court that our belongings are too dangerous to handle, while telling residents that they must take those same belongings and leave. 11. While the City claims the encampment must be cleared to address Leptospirosis, Alameda County Vector Control is actively mitigating the rodent population with residents in place. Moreover, the City’s “Notice of Encampment Closure” is particularly troubling because it omits any advisory about the right to request ADA accommodations. Previous public notices last year included this information. Its deletion means that disabled residents who do not know about the interactive process—who are not connected to BHU—will have no idea that they have rights under the ADA. They will simply be swept, arrested, or both, without ever knowing they could have requested help. 14. Right now, if the City does the sweep in the manner proposed in the notice, where they do not give people a safe place to go, they dispose of all belongings without storing anything, and they arrest people who stay past the notice date, me and my neighbors will face immediate and devastating harm. My neighbors will face arrest and lose everything. My community will be scattered with nowhere to go. If people return to the camp, they will also be subject to citation and arrest without any further notice—a perpetual cycle of punishment with no path to compliance. I attach a copy of an email thread I received that was sent from City Councilmember Rashi Kesarwani’s office that outlines the City’s actions as Exhibit C.
