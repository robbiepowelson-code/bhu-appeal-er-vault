  1. I have personal knowledge of the facts described herein. If called as a witness, I could and would testify competently and truthfully to the facts set forth in this declaration.
  2. I have a Juris Doctor degree from the University of California, Davis—School of Law. I am also a Certified ADA Coordinator through the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) Coordinator Training Certification Program (“ACTCP”) at the Great Plains ADA Center. This certification verifies that I have completed training in ADA regulations and guidelines.
  3. I am the City of Berkeley (“City”) ADA Program Coordinator. My duties include receiving and responding to requests made by members of the public who require reasonable accommodations to access City programs, activities, and services. Any documents or correspondence referred to in this declaration are kept by the City in the regular course of business, and it is a regular practice to make and retain such records.
  4. In my capacity as ADA Program Coordinator, I regularly receive and evaluate requests for reasonable accommodation under Title II of the ADA and related state law and am authorized by the City of Berkeley to make determinations as to whether requests for accommodations are reasonable under the ADA. In doing so, I assess whether requested modifications are reasonable or whether they would fundamentally alter a City program, impose an undue administrative or fiscal burden, or require the City to create or develop an entirely new program or service. This evaluation is conducted consistent with the requirements of Title II and its implementing regulations, including 28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7).
  5. In evaluating accommodation requests, it is my normal practice to consult with relevant City departments and staff in order to understand the operational impact that a requested accommodation would have on the City’s programs and personnel. That information is important in determining whether a requested accommodation would impose an undue burden or fundamentally alter a City program.

For example, requests for sanctioned encampments or designated encampment sites would require the City to create a new program involving land designation, sanitation infrastructure, security, and ongoing management. Is my opinion that simply by establishing a sanctioned encampment, it would itself become a City program, benefit, or service, necessarily requiring the City to provide various types of assistance, services, and staffing to be compliant with the ADA. The ADA does not require a public entity to create entirely new housing or encampment programs as an accommodation, and these measures go beyond what could reasonably be considered an individualized ADA accommodation. Further, requests to remain indefinitely at the Harrison Corridor encampment would have required the City to suspend its encampment management and public health response programs in the presence of confirmed leptospirosis conditions, which in my opinion would fundamentally alter those programs and undermine the City’s ability to address urgent public health hazards. 24. Requests for individualized sanitation services, storage, or moving assistance would require the City to expose City staff to public health risks associated with handling belongings likely contaminated with Leptospira bacteria.
