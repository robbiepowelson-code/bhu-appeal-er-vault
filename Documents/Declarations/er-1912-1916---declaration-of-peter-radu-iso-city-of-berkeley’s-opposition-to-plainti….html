A true and correct copy of the December 12, 2024 memorandum from me to City Manager Paul Buddenhagen Re: Nuisance Declaration and Abatement of Conditions in the Harrison Corridor Encampments is attached hereto as Exhibit A. 6. Based on these documented findings of the dangerous nuisance conditions, on December 18, 2024, Berkeley City Manager Paul Buddenhagen ordered a full closure of Harrison Corridor encampment. 7. The Berkeley Municipal Code requires the City allow for a hearing when a health hazard and nuisance is declared. Therefore, notices were posted on January 7, 2025, informing residents that the Harrison Corridor encampment would be subject to closure at any time after February 10, 2025 in order to allow time for everyone to prepare for the hearing, for the hearing officer to issue a ruling, and for people to have a reasonable amount of time after the ruling to comply if the declaration was upheld. Posting notices more than one month before the compliance deadline was also intended to provide sufficient time for individuals with disability-related needs to raise any reasonable accommodation requests to the City. 8. On January 29, 2025, the City’s Administrative Hearing Officer issued a ruling overruling the appellants objections and affirming the City’s ability to conduct a nuisance abatement, and the City posted a second notice on January 31, 2025. 9. The City personnel who work on and the equipment used for encampment closures are not dedicated solely to this task. Staff schedules are often filled providing critical city services many weeks and months in advance. Scheduling a large operation like an encampment closure, where many staff and equipment are pulled from across multiple departments, necessarily comes with an opportunity cost of foregoing other work and performing other City services. Rescheduling an encampment closure can have a cascading effect on other city operations. For example, pulling a backhoe from a time-bound construction project can lead to delays, change orders, and costly overruns. 11. In my experience, publicizing the exact date of an encampment closure invites a large number of people to protest at the event, which can create safety issues in crowd control and management for everyone. Moreover, the nuisance abatement procedures specified in Berkeley Municipal Code (“BMC”) City “shall have the power to abate such nuisance without further notice including the power to condemn and destroy any property constituting the nuisance if the nuisance cannot be abated without destruction of such property.” BMC § 11.40.110. 1 This section of the BMC was provided as advanced notice to the encampment residents in the January 7, 2025 and January 31, 2025 public notices posted throughout the Harrison Corridor.
