Under Title II of the ADA, the City may deny requests based on fundamental alteration or undue burden, because of the impact that a particular request would have on the City and its programs or services. As I have previously informed you, those impacts to the City are the same, regardless of the individual’s disability or the nexus for their request. As such, even if there were a nexus between Mr. White’s requests and his required relocation, the following determinations would still apply. The determinations below, including alternatives where feasible, were made by me in consultation with the City Manager’s Office and relevant City departments and staff. Re your first enumerated request (“City-Allocated Lot Camping Space”), no sanctioned campsite exists in Berkeley at this time. The City’s encampment regulations generally prohibit structures exceeding nine square feet on sidewalks and prohibit lodging or storage of property in certain other locations, including medians and City building curtilage. Individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding nine square feet, provided pedestrian access is maintained and the area is not designated as a no-lodging zone. Even then, all individuals remain subject to enforcement based on health and safety conditions. Following posted signage and applicable codes and regulations when relocating will reduce the likelihood of enforcement in a subsequent location, although enforcement decisions remain condition dependent. If Mr. White is seeking assistance or services, he may contact HRT or visit https://berkeleyca.gov/safetyhealth/homeless-services for information about homeless services that may be available to him. Re your eighth enumerated request (“Non-Congregate Shelter Placement”), shelter placement is not itself a reasonable accommodation under Title II of the ADA. The provision of shelter is a public service that may be available depending on eligibility, capacity, and prioritization criteria. The ADA requires reasonable modifications to policies to ensure equal access to an existing program; it does not require the creation of new housing resources or the guarantee of shelter. Once Mr. White is offered shelter through the City’s Homeless Response Team (HRT), he may request reasonable modifications related to the shelter environment itself, and those would be evaluated in that context. Mr. White is encouraged to contact HRT to learn about currently available non-congregate shelter opportunities. He may also contact 211 for information regarding regional resources. Re your ninth enumerated request (“ADA-Exempted Footprint”), the request for an indefinite or permanent footprint on any public sidewalk, even provided a four-foot pedestrian clearance is maintained, cannot be granted. Public sidewalks serve multiple legally protected purposes, including pedestrian travel (and associated accessibility compliance obligations), emergency access, commercial activity conducted under active City permits, and placement of utilities and public infrastructure. Sidewalk widths vary considerably, and in many locations a four-foot clearance would not satisfy accessibility requirements once curb ramps, turning radii, and other obstructions are considered. Granting Mr. White an exemption from the nine-square-foot limitation would fundamentally alter the City’s management of the public right-of-way and conflict with other legally protected uses.
