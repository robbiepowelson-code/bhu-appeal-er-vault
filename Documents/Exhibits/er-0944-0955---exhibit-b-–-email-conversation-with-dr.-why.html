Berkeley Homeless Union berkeley.homeless.union@gmail.com Request for Assistance: Rodent crisis at 8th & Harrison St encampment 9 messages Berkeley Homeless Union berkeley.homeless.union@gmail.com Tue, Feb 3, 2026 at 10:20 AM To: adena.why@acgov.org Hi Ms. Why, I hope you are doing well. It was a pleasure to meet you last Wednesday during our community clean up. I am writing on behalf of the Berkeley Homeless Union and residents of the encampment at 8th Street and Harrison Street, regarding the escalating rodent crisis. Our organization is reaching out in the hope of securing your agency’s effective intervention. Summary of the situation The rodent infestation originates from two adjacent private commercial properties where severe harborage conditions have been left unaddressed for years:

  1. 1201 Eighth Street (Hathaway Dinwiddie Construction): Overgrown vegetation and active rat burrows behind its fence.
  2. 1150 Eighth Street (USPS Detached Delivery Unit): Active rat burrows behind its fence. These fenced, private parcels have created an undisturbed, permanent habitat where rats live and breed without any human or animal activity to displace them. This population then migrates into the surrounding encampment and neighborhood. Despite multiple formal complaints and a public health alert for Leptospirosis, the City of Berkeley’s Vector Control Program has taken no action and has not responded to our requests for service. The last documented rodent treatment in the area was on July 21, 2023, and the population has been left unchecked since then (see attached CO2 log). We are in urgent need of assistance. As you mentioned your program was established to help encampments, so we hope we can work together to mitigate this situation. Our request We understand that Alameda County Vector Control may have resources, expertise and the ability to coordinate with businesses in the area. I am glad you have the permission of the Post Office to mitigate conditions there, but because they are being trapped there, more rodents are migrating to the construction yard property. I have attached the contact of the owner, which is listed on their encroachment permit, if it would facilitate coordinating with him. We urgently request your assistance in one or more of the following ways:
  3. Direct assessment: Could your agency conduct an independent inspection of the rodent harborage conditions at these private properties? (I have attached photos for your reference of the conditions in these properties.) We have not yet seen traps deployed around the full perimeter of the Post Office or the construction yard, which we believe would help significantly reduce migration.
  4. Technical assistance: Could you provide guidance and support for an effective integrated pest management plan, focusing on trapping and burrow treatment at these properties? I am happy to work with your staff on-site to identify new burrows and access points. I am happy to discuss this further when I have a better understanding of where the traps are to be used. As I mentioned to you previously, keeping any food in the encampment contained and not left out in the open overnight, as well as storing all dog food in secured plastic/metal containers will be most helpful to help control the rodent population. No problem, I appreciate you getting back to me. Thank you so much for your detailed update and for the important work your team is doing. We are grateful for the weekly effort. I am available this coming Monday, February 9, and would be happy to meet on-site. And to address your specific questions and provide some clarity from our side: Everyone is doing their best to keep all food in secured containers and off the ground. We have not had rats get inside the shelter vehicles (RVs/vans). The primary concern is our proximity to the active burrows directly adjacent to where we are parked. We understand and share your concern about snap traps with free-roaming animals. In our immediate section, which are 6 shelter vehicles on Eighth street, there are no free-roaming dogs or puppies. My cat and my neighbor’s dogs also stay indoors. This makes our area a good candidate for strategically placed traps to protect our dwellings. One of my neighbors has cleared vegetation and crushed burrows near his RV to deter them. However, the rats simply retreat to their undisturbed shelters on the private properties and return—it feels like a game of “whack-amole.” One recently made a burrow in a plant pot by my RV. On the construction yard, it would help tremendously if traps could also be set inside the yard as it’s a vast open space. I see the rats run in and out of that fenced property, and there may be additional harborage within the yard itself beyond the burrows we can see outside near us. Gaining access there could significantly reduce the population circulating into our area. We are ready to support your efforts in any way we can. Please let me know a good time to connect on Monday, and if there is anything we can do to help during your team’s visit. Thank you again for your action.
