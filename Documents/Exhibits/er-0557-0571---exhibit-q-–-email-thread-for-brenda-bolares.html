Berkeley Homeless Union berkeley.homeless.union@gmail.com REQUEST: Disability Accommodation for Brenda Bolares Berkeley Homeless Union berkeley.homeless.union@gmail.com Mon, Feb 3, 2025 at 4:16 PM To: “Gregory, Thomas” tgregory@berkeleyca.gov Cc: “brandyboman24@gmail.com” brandyboman24@gmail.com Dear Mr. Gregory, I am writing on behalf of Brenda Bolares, a member of the Berkeley Homeless Union, to formally request a reasonable accommodation under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) due to her learning and cognitive disabilities. Ms. Bolares faces significant challenges in completing daily life tasks in an outdoor environment, particularly those that require problem-solving, decision-making, and adaptation to frequent change. Due to her disability-related impairments, relocating would create an unreasonable hardship and exacerbate her difficulties in maintaining stability, accessing resources, and performing essential functions. Requested ADA Accommodations:

  1. Permission to remain in place with her tent until an ADA-compliant shelter is available – Due to her cognitive disability, frequent displacement prevents her from maintaining stability and worsens her ability to function independently.
  2. Exemption from relocation requirements that would force her into an unstable and overwhelming situation – Relocation increases her cognitive overload, making it nearly impossible for her to perform basic survival tasks.
  3. Protection of her tent as a necessary accommodation – Her tent provides a structured, familiar environment that helps her manage her disability-related challenges in an already difficult situation.
  4. Exemption from citation and arrest related to the abatement order – Because her disability prevents her from complying with relocation orders and her dwelling was not documented in the city’s abatement report as imposing a health and safety concern, enforcing penalties against her would constitute disability-based discrimination under Title II of the ADA. Legal Basis for Accommodation Request Under Title II of the ADA (28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7)), public entities must reasonably modify policies, practices, or procedures to accommodate individuals with disabilities unless the city can demonstrate that the modification would fundamentally alter the nature of the program or impose an undue burden. City’s Violation if Denied: Frequent displacement disrupts her ability to function, worsening her cognitive struggles and difficulty with problem-solving in an unstable environment. Removing her tent eliminates her only stable and disability-adapted shelter, which directly impacts her ability to manage daily life tasks. Issuing citations or arresting her for failing to relocate would penalize her for a disability-related limitation, violating ADA protections against discrimination. The city has not provided an alternative ADA-compliant shelter, meaning forced relocation would place her life at severe risk without a suitable alternative available.
