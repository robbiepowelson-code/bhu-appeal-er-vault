Requested Accommodations a) Interim Housing Placement (hotel or private non-congregate unit) The ADA requires reasonable modifications to policies where necessary to avoid disability-based discrimination. The City maintains that the provisions of shelter or housing itself is not a form of reasonable accommodation under the ADA. However, once a City-sponsored shelter placement becomes available, the City is obligated to consider reasonable accommodations related to the shelter environment itself, to ensure equal access to that program. When shelter placement is offered to Mr. Holder, he may request a disability-related modification in that context. At present, the ADA does not require the City to provide a specific housing outcome, such as a privately billed hotel room in another jurisdiction, or to bypass established shelter eligibility and prioritization systems absent a showing that those systems deny equal access because of disability. Shelter options are available that would accept his service animal. I understand from prior communications that Mr. Holder has declined both congregate and non-congregate shelter options within the City and has expressed interest only in permanent supportive housing and a private hotel room in San Francisco in the interim. I continue to encourage him to engage with HRT to explore available options and to request modifications in that context if needed. b) Designated Enforcement-Free Relocation Area Case 3:25-cv-01414-EMC Document 272-8 Filed 03/11/26 Page 6 of 9 ER-488 Case: 26-2755, 07/27/2026, DktEntry: 30.4, (88 of 235)

There are no enforcement-free areas within the City. The City reiterates that it does not maintain sanctioned encampment areas and that its encampment regulations are reflected in municipal code provisions, administrative regulations, and City Council resolutions that apply citywide and are subject to change. The City’s encampment-related requirements are established through a combination of municipal code provisions, administrative regulations, and City Council resolutions, all of which may be updated in response to public health conditions, safety concerns, and legal requirements. For example: Park rules prohibit overnight camping and unattended property exceeding ten square feet without a permit. The Berkeley Municipal Code and Administrative Regulation 10.2 generally prohibit structures exceeding nine square feet on sidewalks. Those provisions also prohibit lodging or storage of property on medians and within City building curtilage. In limited circumstances, individuals may remain on certain sidewalks with property not exceeding nine square feet, provided pedestrian access is maintained, facilities are not obstructed, and the location is not designated as a no-lodging area. Even in those circumstances, individuals remain subject to enforcement based on health and safety impacts, nuisance conditions, and compliance with applicable law. Because enforcement determinations are location-specific, condition-dependent, and responsive to changing circumstances, including sanitation, obstruction, emergency access, and public health factors, it is not possible for the City to identify locations where camping would be categorically free from enforcement. Creating a designated enforcement-free zone for one individual would require suspending generally applicable municipal code provisions and administrative regulations in a defined geographic area. That would constitute a fundamental alteration of the City’s regulatory framework and is not required by the ADA. As an alternative, I have previously provided a summary of applicable rules. c) 30 Days’ Advance Written Notice of Any Enforcement The request for a guaranteed 30-day advance written notice before any future enforcement action seeks a categorical modification of the City’s enforcement timelines. The City already provides notice periods that exceed minimum constitutional and statutory requirements. These extended notice periods are intended, in part, to account for the fact that individuals with disabilities may require additional time to review, process, and comply. Imposing a mandatory 30-day delay in all circumstances would:

  1. Create an Undue Administrative Burden – The City must respond to evolving conditions, including blocked rights of way, fire hazards, infrastructure damage, and sanitation failures. A fixed 30-day delay would require the City to suspend standard enforcement timelines and track individualized notice guarantees across changing conditions, which would not be operationally feasible on a citywide basis.
  2. Fundamentally Alter Enforcement Authority – The City’s health and safety enforcement framework depends on the ability to respond proportionally and promptly to conditions on the ground. A guaranteed delay, regardless of circumstances, would substantially alter how the City carries out its legal obligations. As an alternative, I have provided summaries of applicable rules. e) Recognition of Tent as Medical Equipment The City does not intend to seize or destroy Mr. Holder’s tent. The purpose of advance notice is to allow voluntary relocation before any abatement activity occurs. The ADA does not require the City to designate a tent located on public property as medical equipment exempt from generally applicable health and safety laws. However, consistent with established practice, the City provides notice specifically to allow individuals to move with their belongings intact. Mr. Holder has been aware of the abatement process since at least February 5 and has been repeatedly encouraged to engage with HRT regarding relocation and shelter options. f) Physical Assistance with Relocation You state that Mr. Holder requires assistance to avoid physical strain. However, no physical mobility impairment or lifting limitation has been identified, and Mr. Holder has not previously indicated such limitations in our discussions. A reasonable accommodation requires a clear nexus between the disability and the requested modification. As such, that nexus has not been established. g) Temporary Stay-in-Place The City cannot grant a temporary pause in enforcement at this location. The encampment conditions contributing to the leptospirosis-related public health concerns must be addressed. Allowing continued occupation at this site would delay mitigation of sanitation and hinder the ability of the city to address the extensive rodent population that is the primary vector for leptospirosis. Where a requested modification would prevent the City from addressing conditions that pose a direct threat to health and safety, the ADA does not require that modification. Given the length of prior notice and outreach, there is no ADA-based basis for extending occupancy at this location in a manner that would interfere with public health response. h) ADA-Exempted 12’×12’ Footprint on Public Sidewalks The request for a permanent 140-square-foot footprint on any public sidewalk, provided a four-foot pedestrian path is maintained, cannot be granted. Pedestrian travel, including accessibility compliance obligations; Emergency and fire access; Commercial activity conducted under active and ongoing City permits (including outdoor dining, vending, retail displays, and other authorized uses); Placement of utilities, street furniture, and other public infrastructure. Sidewalk width varies considerably throughout the City. In many locations, maintaining only a four-foot clearance would not satisfy accessibility requirements once curb ramps, obstructions, turning radii, and permitted commercial installations are accounted for.
