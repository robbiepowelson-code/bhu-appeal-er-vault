Office of the City Manager To: Paul Buddenhagen, City Manager From: Peter Radu, Assistant to the City Manager Neighborhood Services Date: December 12, 2024 Re: Nuisance Declaration and Abatement of Conditions in the Harrison Corridor Encampments HISTORY OF ENGAGEMENT AND SERVICE PROVISION EFFORTS The encampments in and along Harrison St, 8th St, and the surrounding areas in West Berkeley (referred to herein as have received the most attention, from a services and intervention standpoint, of any encampment in the City of Berkeley. Since September 2021, when the Homeless Response Team (HRT) was launched, the HRT has made 738 shelter offers to encampment residents, and over one in 5 of those have been to people living in these encampments. The HRT has performed 18 encampment operations, such as deep cleanings and closures, in the Harrison Corridor, and this represents over one in 5 of the total operations performed to date, citywide. Every closure performed in these encampments has come with shelter offers for people living there; importantly, the City of Berkeley which is one of only three jurisdictions in the entire State, along with Oakland and Los Angeles, to receive funding received roughly $5M in ERF-2 funding to master lease the Campus Motel on University Avenue and operate it as low-barrier, 24/7, non-congregate shelter, and dedicate it entirely to people living in the Harrison Corridor. This motel as with all our motel-based shelters offer program pets; enroll as a household with their partner; store their possessions; enjoy the privacy of an individual room with a lockable door; and get on a pathway to housing with on-site housing navigation and flexible funds. When we offer non-congregate shelter, HRT data shows that over 4 in 5 people accept it. Even despite this long service provision history, the city was sued in September 2023, and then again in December 2023, to enjoin us from our efforts to bring these vulnerable people out of the dangerous and inhumane conditions in these encampments. Ultimately, the uncertainty associated with ongoing legal proceedings led to a difficult decision by the City, in February of towards other encampment areas in the City. Office of the City Manager To: Dee Williams-Ridley, City Manager From: Peter Radu, Assistant to the City Manager Date: August 31, 2023 Re: Nuisance Declaration and Summary Abatement of Conditions on Harrison St INTRODUCTION AND HISTORY OF SERVICE EFFORTS The encampments in and around Harrison Street, especially those between 8th and 7th Streets, in West Berkeley have presented ongoing challenges to the City. The City has provided a wide range of services for the residents in this area as well as full scale operations to reduce the debris and dangerous conditions. Since September 2021, when the City first resumed encampment operations after a COVID hiatus, these include: The placement of a dumpster on 8th St in April 2022, with service 4 times a week, and a second dumpster on 7th and Harrison in August 2023, as well as weekly illegal dumping removal ; The placement of two port-a-potties with wash station located at the corner of Eighth and Harrison St; both are serviced on a regular basis. Free mobile shower and laundry service to the residents of this encampment on a weekly basis since June 2022. A major effort to relocate RVs in the area in September and October 2021 to Safe RV parking program; multiple offers of an off-street parking spot were offered to all 35 vehicles being used as shelter in the encampment, and 18 moved in or voluntarily relocated. One full closure of the encampment, in February 2022, and 3 partial closures since that time. The full closure in Feb 2022 included motel room offers for everyone living there, using Alameda County Winter Shelter motel vouchers and with an 83% uptake rate. Only two arrests and 0 criminal citations have been issued during these operations. Nine deep cleanings of the encampment, in which a total of 124.9 tons (249,800 pounds) of debris have been removed, and a major nuisance abatement of rodent harborage conditions in October 2022, in which a total of 32.1 tons (64,200 pounds, or nearly the weight of a humpback whale) of debris were removed. network of rodent burrows in and around the encampment, and the provision on numerous occasions of new tents and Sterilite tubs to help maintain cleaner and more rodent-proof conditions.
