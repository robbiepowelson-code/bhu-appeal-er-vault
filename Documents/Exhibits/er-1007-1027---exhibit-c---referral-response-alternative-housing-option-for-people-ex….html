With that said:  If Council’s desire is to replicate a Grayson-style shelter program, then staff would recommend initially performing due diligence at properties that are designated as “warehouses” in Attachment 2 before expanding to consider other property types, such as retail properties.  In addition, if the desire is to replicate other models deemed particularly successful by the City Manager, staff would recommend performing due diligence at both of the motel/hotel properties in Attachment 2, as these properties are most immediately amenable to noncongregate shelter. In the Homeless Response Team’s experience, these two program types have been more successful than traditional congregate shelter and safe parking in resolving encampments and connecting people living in them to permanent housing. POSSIBLE FUTURE ACTION The City Council could use this list to provide staff with further direction as to acceptable locations for new programs and program types that should be prioritized for consideration. In addition, Council could direct staff to pursue one or more new homeless programs at any of the sites listed above, and/or direct staff to return with more or different sites. At this time, staff is pursuing two sites for noncongregate interim housing, and both would be funded by external grants: the Sather Motel, a 32-room motel at 1820 University Ave. which would be leased by County Measure W funds, , as well as another motel to be leased by the City’s recently awarded Prop 47 grant. More information on these opportunities is provided immediately below. If these funding applications are successful, staff will return to Council as soon as possible for all necessary approvals. With additional direction from Council as to which site(s) to pursue, staff can begin to develop estimated program expenses. However, and as previously reported to Council in staff’s July 29 worksession report, “Comprehensive Summary of Berkeley’s Homeless Response,” the City is currently facing a significant and structural fiscal challenge, with ongoing general fund deficits projected at over $20M annually, and state and federal sources that currently support Berkeley’s existing programs are at risk under the current federal administration and economic environment. Absent new sources of external funding, including and possibly Alameda County Measure W, staff do not recommend opening new programs when several existing shelter programs face imminent risk of closure in the coming years. Staff continue to aggressively pursue all available opportunities for external funding. As mentioned above, these include the following two opportunities, neither of which would impact the City’s General Fund as neither would require local match funding:

  1. In June 2025, City staff applied for an $8M grant through the State of California’s Proposition 47 Grant Program. This is a no-match grant. Council voted to accept the funds on Sept 30, 2025. Proposition 47 provides funding for public agencies to provide mental health services, substance use disorder treatment, and diversion programs for individuals in the criminal justice system. The City’s award will create three years of the Berkeley CareBridge Program, providing post-arrest supportive transitional housing and wraparound services for homeless, justiceinvolved individuals. The housing component would be provided through a master lease of a motel facility. The grant also requires establishment of a Local Advisory Committee that will meet publicly on a quarterly basis to provide program updates, hear from the community, and address needs.
  2. In August, 2025, Alameda County released a notice of contract opportunity to nonprofit partners in the County’s homeless system vendor pool, announcing an initiative to rapidly increase new interim housing units that can be used to support encampment resolution. City staff worked with Episcopal Community Services (ECS), a San Francisco-based nonprofit with extensive experience in homeless services, to sponsor ECS’ application for this funding which, if awarded, would fund the master-lease of another motel (the Sather Motel, at 1820 University Avenue) to create a noncongregate shelter. Staff and ECS proposed that this shelter be used to target the large RV encampments in West Berkeley along Dwight and Grayson, using existing Measure P allocations for an expanded RV buy-back program. Importantly, the City would not be a direct recipient of these grant funds, nor would it hold/be party to any motel leases or services contracts, all of which will be handled directly by Alameda County. BACKGROUND On May 20, 2025, the City Council passed a referral to the City Manager, “Alternative Housing Options for People Experiencing Homelessness.” This referral had multiple parts, and the current report is responsive to one part of this referral. Staff actions in response to other aspects of this referral include:  On May 28, the City Manager sent a letter on behalf of the Council to the Alameda County Board of Supervisors, affirming the four policy principles outlined in the May 20, 2025 referral.  On July 24, 2025, Council voted to support staff recommendations in the report, “Recommendations for County Measure W Funding.” The adoptions of these recommendations resulted in a letter sent to the Alameda County Board of Supervisors outlining Berkeley’s priorities for County Measure W sales tax funding.  On July 29, 2025, at a special meeting, Council received a report from staff, “Comprehensive Summary of Berkeley’s Homeless Response System.” This report responded to several aspects of Council’s May 20, 2025 referral, including an overview of ongoing efforts to address homelessness; a resource needs and gaps analysis of the homeless system; a feasibility and cost analysis to support new homeless programs; and a geographic assessment of homeless service locations in the city. Due to technical difficulties, that special meeting was adjourned prior to the conclusion of staff’s presentation on the report. The presentation was concluded during a special meeting on September 16, 2025.  On October 28, 2025, the Council will consider a recommendation from the City Manager to send a letter to Alameda County Supervisor Fortunato Bas regarding opportunities to partner on regional solutions to unsheltered homelessness.
