INTRODUCTION The Court should deny Plaintiff Berkeley Homeless Union’s (“BHU’s”) second Ex Parte Motion For Temporary Restraining Order And Order To Show Cause Why City Of Berkeley And ADA Coordinator Thomas Gregory Should Not Be Held In Contempt, ECF No. 264. Defendant City of Berkeley (“City”) first noticed the closure of the Harrison Street encampment in January 2025 and at that time began preparing in earnest for the partial closure that was noticed for March 9, 2026. Those preparations included extensive public health outreach to address the presence of leptospirosis (a disease caused by bacteria) at the encampment and a prior notice of partial abatement posted in February 2026, which was rescinded by the City due to the expectation of inclement weather at the time of the abatement action. On March 10, in response to Plaintiff’s second TRO Application, the Court issued an order enjoining the City from taking any abatement actions against certain individuals who submitted ADA accommodation requests pending hearing on BHU’s second TRO application, set for March 11 at 11:00 a.m. The Court has already rejected Plaintiff’s first attempt to seek an ex parte application to block the noticed partial abatement. As with its first TRO attempt, the Court should deny this second TRO application because, as it previously observed, Plaintiff has failed to submit evidence that “the City has failed to satisfy its obligations under the ADA or that any particular accommodation request has been unlawfully denied.” ECF No. 260. Plaintiff once again fails to meet its burden of proof in this second, procedurally improper application. Plaintiff submits only 7 declarations in support of its application, far fewer than the many individuals who have sought accommodation. Plaintiff fails to raise any substantive objections to the City’s responses to reasonable accommodation made by encampment residents under the Americans with Disability Act (“ADA”). Instead, without citing authority, they appear to argue that it violates federal law to have the City’s ADA Coordinator send out ADA determinations following outreach and an interactive process. This argument has no merit. Plaintiff submits declarations that ignore the City’s extensive efforts to review and respond to Plaintiff’s requests for accommodation, as explained in detail in the Declaration of the City’s ADA Coordinator, Thomas Gregory (“Gregory Decl.”) ECF No. 261. LEGAL STANDARD A temporary restraining order is “an extraordinary remedy that may only be awarded upon a clear showing that the plaintiff is entitled to such relief.” Winter v. Nat. Res. Def. Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7, 22 (2008). In the Ninth Circuit, the same standard governs requests for temporary restraining orders and preliminary injunctions. See Stuhlbarg Int’l Sales Co. v. John D. Brush & Co., 240 F.3d 832, 839 n.7 (9th Cir. 2001). The Court Should Reject Plaintiff’s Procedurally Improper Ex Parte Application. Local Rule 65-1 establishes the procedures for filing a motion seeking a temporary restraining order. In pertinent part, the Rule requires (1) separate memorandum of points and authorities in support of the motion, (2) a proposed order and order to show cause, and (3) a declaration by counsel certifying that notice has been provided to the opposing party or explaining why such notice could not be provided. N.D Cal. L.R. 65-1. Plaintiff complied with none of these requirements. See EFC No. 264. Plaintiff’s ex parte application is improper. See N.D. Cal. L.R. 7-1 (“Unless otherwise ordered by the assigned Judge, a party may file an ex parte motion, that is, a motion filed without notice to opposing party, only if a statute, Federal Rule, local rule, or Standing Order authorizes ex parte filing.”). Plaintiff’s filing of a last-minute application is highly prejudicial to the City. The Court should enforce the plain language of its procedural rules and deny Plaintiff’s application for failing to comply with them. B. Plaintiff Cannot Prevail On The Merits Of Its ADA Claim. Plaintiff’s second application for a temporary restraining order fails to address the factual or legal grounds for the City’s responses to the newly identified ADA claimants. Both of these defects provide alternative grounds for denying Plaintiff’s application.
