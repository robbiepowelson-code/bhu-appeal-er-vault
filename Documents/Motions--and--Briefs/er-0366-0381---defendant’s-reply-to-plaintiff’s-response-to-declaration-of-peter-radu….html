The City has not met that requirement. The City has merely offered a general assertion that stationary vehicles may harbor rodent nests or impede abatement efforts. The City Can Lawfully Tow Inoperable Vehicles or Those with Expired Registrations Plaintiffs do not dispute that the four vehicles the City intends to remove are either inoperable, unregistered, or both, and as such there is no Fourth Amendment violation associated with their lawful towing. While the Fourth Amendment protects people and their effects “against unreasonable searches and seizures,” there are recognized exceptions for warrantless tows. (See Cady v. Dombrowski, 413 U.S. 433 (1973).) In particular, police officers are justified in impounding vehicles that “jeopardize [] the public safety.” (Opperman, 428 U.S. at 369.) An impoundment may be proper if its condition prevents the driver from lawfully operating the vehicle or if necessary to remove the vehicle from an exposed or public location. (Miranda, 429 F.3d at 865.) Importantly, the Supreme Court has made clear that “[t]he authority of police to seize and remove from the streets vehicles impeding traffic or threatening public safety and convenience is beyond challenge.” (Id. (emphasis added).) Pursuant to the City’s abatement notice and no parking signs, the City intends to tow four vehicles—an RV, a sedan, a station wagon, and a minivan. (Radu Declaration ¶ 6.) Three of those vehicles have expired registrations (the sedan, station wagon, and minivan) and three are inoperable (the RV, sedan, and station wagon). (Id.) Beginning with the unregistered vehicles, the California Vehicle code provides that “[i]f a vehicle is found or operated upon a highway, public land… [w]ith a registration expiration date in excess of six months before the date it is found” an officer may remove the vehicle. (Cal. Veh. Code § 22651(o)(A)(i).) Plaintiffs do not contest that the station wagon and minivan’s registrations are both more than six months out of date. (ECF No. 199.) Courts have consistently found that a warrantless towing of a car without a valid registration is permitted because leaving it would otherwise create a risk that the car would be driven while unregistered, which is also a violation of the California Vehicle Code.1 (See, e.g., Halajian v.
