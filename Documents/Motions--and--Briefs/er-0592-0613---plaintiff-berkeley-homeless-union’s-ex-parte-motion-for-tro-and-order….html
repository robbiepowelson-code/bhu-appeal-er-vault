Yesica Prado has served as President of BHU since July 2024 and regularly advocates for unhoused residents in Berkeley, including those residing at the Harrison Street encampment. On February 17, 2026, the City of Berkeley posted notices announcing the closure of the encampment located at 8th and Harrison Streets. The City’s planned action would displace unhoused people there and destroy their property, based on an alleged bacterial infection around the camp. This first sweep was postponed based on a rainstorm that was coming around the same time as the planned eviction. In response, BHU began assisting encampment residents in submitting requests for disability-related accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”). Starting around February 18, Yesica Prado emailed around 16 disability accommodation requests to ADA Coordinator Thomas Gregory, asking for reasonable accommodations pertaining to the eviction. In total, there were 18 requests for reasonable accommodations under the ADA filed with the city of Berkeley before the encampment abatement deadline. Two were submitted through a nonprofit organization, Where Do We Go? Additional residents have also requested assistance with accommodation requests that have not yet been submitted. The Harrison Street encampment currently houses approximately 40 unhoused individuals. Based on Prado’s regular presence at the encampment over the past eight years, approximately eighty percent of the residents have disabilities that substantially limit one or more major life activities. These disabilities include post-traumatic stress disorder, traumatic brain injury, mobility impairments, chronic pain conditions, autoimmune disorders, heart conditions, seizure disorders, memory impairment, dementia, schizophrenia, severe anxiety and depression, and vision and hearing impairments.
