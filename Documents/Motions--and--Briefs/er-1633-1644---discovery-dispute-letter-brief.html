No. 166 Re: Discovery Dispute Letter Brief Dear Judge Beeler: Despite the deadlines set forth in the Court’s November 30, 2025 Order addressing Discovery Letter Brief, Dkt. No. 182, Defendant City of Berkeley (“City”) is once again in the position of having to submit this unilateral letter brief due to the failure of Plaintiff Berkeley Homeless Union (“BHU”) to engage in the discovery dispute process. A. Defendant’s Opening Brief This dispute arises over BHU’s complete failure to respond to discovery propounded by the City and BHU’s subsequent refusal even to provide a date when responses will be forthcoming. The scheduling order in this case imposes a fact discovery cut-off on February 5, 2026. Dkt. No. 183. BHU’s baseless two-month delay in responding to the City’s initial discovery requests has deprived the City of time it should have been able to use to prepare its defenses to BHU’s claims. As discussed below, BHU cannot excuse its behavior, and the City is severely prejudiced by BHU’s continued delays. BHU should be required to respond immediately and without objection or face sanctions.

  1. BHU should be required to respond to all pending discovery immediately and without objection. The City respectfully refers the Court to the City’s November 26, 2025 filing on this matter at Dkt. No. 178 and incorporates by reference that filing here in its entirety. In short:  The City’s interrogatories and requests for production of documents were served respectively on September 12 and 26, 2025. There is no dispute that BHU has never responded to either discovery request.  BHU did not seek an extension of time to respond to the interrogatories until after the deadline passed, and BHU never requested an extension of time to produce documents. BHU’s continued refusal to respond to discovery prejudices the City. Not only is it impossible for BHU to justify its utter failure to comply with the most basic discovery obligations, but BHU has also refused to engage in a good faith meet and confer effort. The City sent its portion of a joint discovery dispute letter to counsel for BHU on November 19, 2025; receiving no response from BHU, the City filed is unilateral Discovery Dispute Letter. Dkt. No. 178. Upon receipt of the Court’s November 30, 2025 Order, the City immediately contacted BHU to schedule a further meet and confer within 3 business days.2 Although counsel for BHU initially stated he was available the afternoon of December 8, 2025, when the time came for the phone call he was unprepared to discuss the issue. Instead, the parties met and conferred on December 9, 2025, and the City shortly thereafter provided an updated draft joint discovery dispute letter with a request to receive BHU’s position no later than December 15, 2025. See Exhibit A. On December 15, 2025, BHU’s counsel informed the City via telephone that he would not provide BHU’s discovery dispute position. The parties have a fact discovery cut-off of February 5, 2025. Dkt. No. 183. The City acted diligently to propound discovery in September, entitling it to BHU’s responses well in advance of the cut-off and allowing plenty of time for motion practice, follow-up discovery, and depositions. The City reached out to BHU immediately after BHU’s interrogatory responses were due. See Dkt. No. 178 at 6. However, because BHU has withheld responses for over two months and failed to properly engage in the meet and confer process, the City is now prejudiced with an unreasonably short window within which to complete discovery. 3. BHU’s conduct warrants sanctions. This letter brief is necessary in light of BHU’s serial failure to meet deadlines established by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, the Court’s standing orders, and BHU’s own promises. In light of BHU’s demonstrated bad faith, the Court should not only require BHU to respond immediately to the City’s discovery requests without objection, it should further order that any failure to comply shall result in sanctions pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(d). Such sanctions should include, at a minimum, reasonable expenses, including attorney’s fees, and an order prohibiting BHU from introducing any evidence that should have been produced in response to the City’s pending discovery requests. Defendant’s Reply Brief The City provided the foregoing Defendant’s Opening Brief to BHU on Tuesday, December 16, 2025. Pursuant to the Court’s November 30, 2025 Order, Dkt. No. 182, BHU was required to provide Plaintiff’s responding brief no later than December 18, 2025. On December 18 at 3:38 pm, BHU’s counsel confirmed that he was working on BHU’s response. See Exhibit B. The undersigned counsel for the City reiterated the Court-ordered deadline for BHU to provide its response and stated that the letter brief would be filed on December 19. Id. Counsel for BHU confirmed, “You’ll have it first thing in the morning.” Id. Instead of providing the promised (and required) response, at 7:56 am this morning, BHU’s counsel sent a further email requesting a lastminute extension to provide BHU’s response. Exhibit C. In reality, BHU has been in possession of the City’s written position regarding this discovery dispute for a full month. See Dkt. No. 178 at 1 (“On November 19, 2025, the undersigned sent an email to counsel for Berkeley Homeless Union, attaching a draft joint discovery dispute letter containing the ‘Defendant’s Position’ … .”). Despite having taken for themselves an unreasonable 30 days to respond, and despite finally promising less than twenty four hours ago to do so this morning, BHU has not provided its portion of the letter brief. This most recent failure is not an isolated incident. BHU and its counsel have not only disregarded the Court-imposed deadline to provide a response to the City’s discovery dispute position within 2 days, but also they have failed to meet every other deadline related to discovery and the discovery dispute process since October 2025. BHU’s counsel’s pattern of serially seeking last minute (and after-the-last-minute) extensions for reasons that are unrelated to the litigation demonstrates bad faith and severely prejudices the City’s ability to resolve the current discovery dispute or litigate this case in general. Moreover, so long as there is an injunction in place against the City, BHU and its counsel seemingly have no incentive to move the case to a determination on the merits unless consequences are imposed—here for their failure to abide by the most basic obligations under the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure.3 / / / / / / / / / / / / 3 In addition, to the extent BHU’s attorney’s physical condition “renders it unreasonably difficult to carry out the representation effectively,” he may owe a duty to his client to seek to withdraw from the representation.
