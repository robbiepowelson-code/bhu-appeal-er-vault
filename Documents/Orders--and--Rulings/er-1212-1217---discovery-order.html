Parties may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter that is relevant to any party’s claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case, considering the importance of the issues at stake in the action, the amount in controversy, the parties’ relative access to relevant information, the parties’ resources, the importance of the discovery in resolving the issues, and whether the burden or expense of the proposed discovery outweighs its likely benefit. Information within this scope of discovery need not be admissible in evidence to be discoverable. Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b). “Pretrial discovery is ordinarily accorded a broad and liberal treatment.” Peng v. Nw. Mut. Life Ins. Co., No. 17-cv-01760-SI, 2017 WL 3007030, at *1 (N.D. Cal. July 14, 2017) (cleaned up and quoting Shoen v. Shoen, 5 F.3d 1289, 1292 (9th Cir. 1993)). The party moving to compel discovery “has the initial burden of establishing that the information sought is relevant to any party’s claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case.” Impinj, Inc. v. NXP USA, Inc., No. 19-cv-03161- YGR (AGT), 2022 WL 16586886, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 1, 2022) (cleaned up). First, the court does not order the deposition of Ms. Mattes. Attorney depositions should be permitted only where the party seeking the deposition shows that (1) no other means exist to obtain the information, (2) the information sought is relevant and nonprivileged, and (3) the information is crucial to the preparation of the case. Shelton v. Am. Motors Corp., 805 F.2d 1323, 1327 (8th Cir. 1986); Mass. Mut. Life Ins. Co. v. Cerf, 177 F.R.D. 472, 479 (N.D. Cal. 1998) (Shelton is “generally regarded as the leading case on attorney depositions”). BHU asserts that Ms. Mattes’s testimony meets the requirements because there will be no way to challenge her narrative of the July 18 interactive-process meeting without her testimony, which 9 See Order – ECF No. 196. Second, the court does not order the deposition of City Manager Buddenhagen. “When a party seeks the deposition of a high-level executive (a so-called ‘apex’ deposition), courts have ‘observed that such discovery creates a tremendous potential for abuse or harassment.’” Jensen v. BNSF Rwy. Co., No. 3:13-cv-05955-HSG (LB), 2015 WL 3662593, at *2 (N.D. Cal. May 19, 2015) (quoting Apple Inc. v. Samsung Elecs. Co., 282 F.R.D. 259, 262–63 (N.D. Cal. 2012)). “[O]nly extraordinary circumstances compel high ranking officials to testify to the reasoning behind their official actions.” Kob v. County of Marin, No. C 07–2211 JL, 2009 WL 3706820, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 3, 2009) (cleaned up). “The court therefore has discretion to limit discovery where the discovery sought can be obtained from some other source that is more convenient, less burdensome, or less expensive.” Jensen, 2015 WL 3662593, at *2 (cleaned up). “In determining whether to allow an apex deposition, courts consider (1) whether the deponent has unique first-hand, non-repetitive knowledge of the facts at issue in the case and (2) whether the party seeking the deposition has exhausted other less intrusive discovery methods.” Id. at *3. “[C]ourts generally refuse to allow the immediate deposition of a high level executive … before the testimony of lower level employees with more intimate knowledge of the case has been secured.” Google Inc. v. BHU contends that, under 28 C.F.R. § 35.204, Mr. Buddenhagen is required to make final determinations about BHU’s requests for reasonable accommodations.12 The City responds that (1) § 35.204 allows Mr. Buddenhagen to designate someone to make decisions, (2) BHU does not address the apex doctrine requirements and cannot satisfy them, pointing to the absence of BHU’s propounding of other written discovery or depositions, and (3) requiring Mr. Buddenhagen to testify would create absurd results and an undue burden on public entities.13 Because BHU did not demonstrate that the information it seeks from Mr. Buddenhagen is unique or unavailable through less intrusive methods (including by deposing lower-level employees), the apex doctrine applies. The court denies the discovery.
